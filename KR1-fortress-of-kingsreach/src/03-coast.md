# The Reaching Coast

<div class="map" markdown="1">
**[MAP 1 — THE REACHING COAST. A sea chart in the old style, printed in pale blue with sepia land and an overlay of 3-mile hexes numbered with their Hex Chronicle subhex addresses (plate 2863 and the edges of plates 2993 and 2994), with a compass rose in the lower right and rhumb lines across the water.]**

The land fills the left (west) and upper part of the chart; the sea fills the right (east) and the bottom. A deep bay, **the Sound**, cuts into the coast in the lower center. **Kingsreach (2863-127)** sits on the high cliffs at the Sound's northern shore, on a promontory pointing southeast, its Sea Gate opening toward the water. A dashed cart track, **the King's Road**, runs north from Kingsreach through 2863-114 to the village of **Hillnest (W10, 2863-101)** on the high downs. A second dashed path loops northeast from the town over the heights (2863-115) and comes down to **the Gullet (G, 2863-128)**, a narrow cove one hex east of the town that opens south onto the Sound; a third runs west through 2863-126 and down to the bay shore at **the Drowned Bell (W8, 2863-138)**, a ruined tower on a sea-stack one hex south-southwest of the town. On the heights just east of the Gullet stands **Hob's Crag (W2, 2863-129)**, and off the point beyond it, three tiny pinnacles mark **the Shrieking Rocks (W3, 2863-130)**. North along the coast, a broken line of reef with a wreck symbol near its outer end — **the Teeth (W1, 2863-104)** — runs out to sea from a rocky headland, and a little beyond it a salt marsh with a mound symbol marks **the Fennmouth (W6, 2863-091)**. Across the Sound, three hexes southeast of the town, lies **Lighthouse Isle** (2863-149, 2863-154, and 2993-034), with **the Kingslight (W9)** drawn as a tall black triangle at its northern tip; **Spider Cove (W7, 2993-021)** is a small cove on the Sound's far southern shore. Further out to sea to the southeast lies a two-hex island: **Gannet Islet (W4, 2863-156)**, wooded, with a wrecked hull on its western beach, and at its scrubby eastern end, **Seal Skerry (W5, 2994-014)**. The high hills west of Hillnest, rising toward 3,000 feet, are hatched and labeled **THE THORNWOLD — TRACKLESS**.
</div>

The waters and shores around Kingsreach are shown on Map 1, the sea chart; each site is given with its Hex Chronicle subhex address, and the table under *Distances* gives travel times. Two paths and the King's Road lead overland from the town (to the Gullet, the Drowned Bell, and Hillnest); everything else must be reached by boat. The sea cliffs of this coast rise hundreds of feet from the water and are unclimbable except where noted, and characters may land on beaches only where they are shown on the chart.

**Wandering Encounters Ashore.** When the party is ashore anywhere on the Reaching Coast (but not in the Gullet's caves), check for a wandering encounter once every 2 turns, rolling 1d6. An encounter occurs on a roll of 1. Roll 1d8:

## Key to the Reaching Coast

**W1. THE TEETH (2863-104).** Three leagues north-northeast of Kingsreach by boat, a broken, jagged line of black reef runs a mile out to sea from a rocky headland, guarding the approach to the coast between the Fennmouth and the Gullet. At low tide the Teeth stand 10' out of the water like a jaw of broken fangs; at high tide only their tips show, and the sea breaks white over them. A boat can be rowed carefully among them in calm weather; in choppy or rough water, a boat that tries it has a 1 in 6 chance per turn of striking a rock and being holed (it sinks in 1d4 rounds). The bones of a dozen wrecks lie on the Teeth, most long since broken up and picked clean. But near the outer end of the reef lies the most recent wreck, the **merchantman *Merrow***, lost six months ago.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
The wreck lies on her side, wedged between two great black rocks, her back broken. Her masts are gone and her planking hangs in strips, green with weed. Waves slap and suck through her gaping hull. On her stern, under the barnacles, you can still make out a carved painted name: *MERROW*.
</div>

The *Merrow* can be entered from a boat at any tide, but her lower hold is flooded at high water. Her deck is slanted 40 degrees and slick with weed; anyone fighting on it must make a Dexterity check each round or fall (1d4 damage and possibly into the sea).

**Upper deck and captain's cabin.** The cabin is a wreck of smashed furniture. The wreckers took everything of obvious value, but missed a hidden compartment in the captain's bunk (found on a 1 in 6, or 1–2 for dwarves and thieves) containing the ship's log in an oilskin wrapper, and 30 pp. The last entry in the log reads: "*Storm rising. Kingslight sighted bearing WSW, fainter than I recall. Making for it.*" Below this, in a shaky hand: "*Two lights?? Breakers ahead —*" The Castellan would value this log greatly (50 gp).

**Lower hold.** At low tide, the hold is knee-deep in water and home to **3 giant crabs**, which attack anyone entering. Among the sodden cargo of rotted cloth are two unbroken casks of fine southern wine (worth 40 gp each, but weighing 300 coins each) and a crushed crate of 200 copper ingots (worth 1 gp each, but weighing 50 coins apiece) that the wreckers left behind as too heavy to carry.

*3 Giant crabs (AC 2, HD 3, hp 16, 12, 9, #AT 2 pincers, D 2d6/2d6, MV 60' (20'), Save F2, ML 7)*

At night, there is a 2 in 6 chance that **4 brine dead** — the *Merrow*'s own drowned sailors, raised by the Tidecaller and left to guard the wreck — will climb out of the water onto the deck. One of them wears Captain Halvard's coat and a gold chain of office worth 150 gp.

**W2. HOB'S CRAG (2863-129).** On the heights just east of the Gullet, above the cove's eastern wall, a fang of bare rock rises 200' sheer from the heather. This is Hob's Crag. No path comes near it: the cliff path from Kingsreach ends at the Gullet, and the crag's summit can be reached only by climbing up through the Gullet's caves (via area D8 or G1), or by a cliff climb that only a thief could attempt (and even then at –20%). From the top, Kingsreach can be seen clearly two leagues to the west, the Kingslight to the south across the Sound, and the line of the Teeth to the north.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
The crag is a jumble of wind-scoured boulders, rising from a sea of heather and gorse. On its highest point stands a great iron basket on a tripod of timbers, black with soot and stuffed with tarred wood and old rope, ready for the torch. A low hut of turf and driftwood crouches in the lee of the rocks below it.
</div>

This is the wreckers' false beacon. Seen from the sea at night, it looks almost exactly like the Kingslight seen from farther off — especially when the true Kingslight has been dimmed. The hut is a watch post manned by **3 wreckers** and a **lookout** with a horn. If they sight an enemy coming up from the Gullet, the lookout blows the horn, which alerts areas D and G.

*3 Wreckers (AC 7, F1, hp 5, 4, 4, #AT 1 short sword, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 7)*
*Lookout (AC 8, T1, hp 3, #AT 1 sling, D 1d4, ML 6)*

In the hut are four bedrolls, a barrel of fresh water, a sack of salt fish, a crock of lamp oil, a horn, and a small carved wooden box holding 23 sp, 6 gp, and a crude chart of the Teeth marked with an X at each of the wrecks. If the beacon is destroyed, the wreckers will be furious, and will build a new one within a week unless Black Maudie has been dealt with.

Characters who cross the heather eastward from Hob's Crag soon reach the edge of the Thornwold, where the briars grow impassably thick. The DM may expand this region as desired.

**W3. THE SHRIEKING ROCKS (2863-130).** Three bare pinnacles of rock rise from the sea off the point east of Hob's Crag, three leagues from Kingsreach by boat, white with the droppings of seabirds. The highest pinnacle is the nest of **3 harpies**, foul bird-women with the bodies of vultures, who sing to passing sailors to lure them onto the rocks. Fishermen give the Rocks a wide berth, but adventurers rowing past within 300 yards will hear their song.

Each character hearing the song must save vs. Spells or be **charmed** — the charmed character will try to row (or swim) toward the Rocks, and will not defend himself against the harpies. The harpies will swoop down to attack anyone who comes close. A boat rowed by charmed characters will run onto the rocks and be holed on a 1–3 on 1d6.

*3 Harpies (AC 7, HD 3*, hp 15, 13, 10, #AT 2 claws/1 weapon, D 1d4/1d4/1d6 + charm song, MV 60' (20'), fly 150' (50'), Save F3, ML 7)*

The harpies' nest, a filthy heap of bones, driftwood, and feathers on the top of the pinnacle (a 40' climb), holds the treasure they have taken from drowned sailors: 340 sp, 210 gp, a pearl necklace worth 500 gp, a silver compass worth 75 gp, and a *potion of water breathing* in a sealed green bottle. This is a very dangerous encounter for 1st-level characters, and the DM should make sure the players have heard rumor 13 or the warnings of the fisherfolk before they blunder near.

**W4. GANNET ISLET AND THE MAD HERMIT (2863-156).** Five leagues southeast of Kingsreach, out beyond Lighthouse Isle, lies a long island crowned with wind-bent pines, where gannets nest by the thousand on the cliffs. On a shingle beach on its western side, facing the distant cliffs of the colony, lies the old hull of a fishing ship, dragged above the high-tide line and turned upside down to make a house. A crooked chimney of stones pokes through its keel. Here lives the **Hermit of Gannet Islet**, a castaway who calls himself **Admiral Kittiwake** (*AC 6 (from his quickness and an old cork-and-leather vest), F3, hp 20, #AT 1 boathook, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save F3, ML 10, AL N*). Kittiwake was once a ship's officer on a vessel wrecked on the Teeth fifteen years ago; he was the only survivor, and the long years alone have cracked his mind.

The Admiral believes the islet is his flagship, that the gannets are his crew, and that he is at war with "the Black Fish" (the Drowned Mother). He will challenge any boat that approaches ("Heave to, and state your colors!"), and the characters' reception depends on how they answer. Roll for the Admiral's reaction, with +2 if the characters address him by rank, salute, or play along; –2 if they laugh at him. On a hostile result, he will hurl stones from the beach and threaten them with his boathook, and his "first mate" — **Duchess**, an enormous, battle-scarred tame sea lion — will charge into the surf to overturn their boat.

*Duchess, sea lion (AC 6, HD 3, hp 18, #AT 1 bite, D 1d8, MV 30' (10') on land, swim 180' (60'), Save F2, ML 9)*

If the reaction is friendly, the Admiral will invite them "aboard" for a dinner of gannet eggs and seaweed, and will talk endlessly. Most of what he says is nonsense, but buried in it are three true and valuable things, which the DM should reveal over the course of the conversation:

1. "The Black Fish keeps her mouth in the deep. A man can swim in at the low spring tide, if he's a fish." (The underwater entrance to the Drowned Chapel, area I12.)
2. "The dead ones can't abide the Saint's lantern-light. I've seen 'em turn tail from the Kingslight itself." (Brine dead suffer from the *Lantern of Saint Elmar*; see New Magic Items.)
3. "Strake's a woman, and she's got a scar from here to here. I seen her on the crag lighting her fire." (Black Maudie's identity.)

The Admiral's "treasure," hidden in a sea chest under his bunk, is a bundle of 112 gp and 40 pp, a fine but tarnished officer's sword in a sharkskin scabbard (a *sword +1*), and a little ivory carving of a ship worth 60 gp. He will not part with the sword unless the characters have truly won his trust — for example, by bringing him proof that "the Black Fish" has been defeated, at which point he will gravely present it to the bravest of them "for distinguished service to the Fleet."

**W5. SEAL SKERRY AND THE MERFOLK (2994-014).** The eastern end of Gannet Islet, a league from the Admiral's beach, is a low, scrubby tail of land that breaks up into a cluster of weedy rocks, where gray seals bask by the hundred. Under the water nearby, among forests of kelp, is a small village of **mermen** — a clan of 30, of whom 12 are warriors. The mermen are neutral, wary, and proud. They have no love for land-folk (who net their fish and take their pearls) but they hate the Drowned Mother's cult far more, for the brine dead have taken several of their young.

If the party approaches peaceably — especially if they bring gifts of iron knives, spearheads, or tools, which the mermen cannot make — **1d6 mermen** will surface to parley, led by the clan's speaker, **Thallo** (*AC 6, HD 2, hp 12, #AT 1 trident, D 1d6+1, MV swim 120' (40'), Save F2, ML 9, AL N*). Thallo speaks a little of the common tongue. With a friendly reaction (+1 for each gift of iron worth 5 gp or more), the mermen will:

* tell the characters that the "dead-who-walk-in-the-sea" come from "the Mother's Throat," a hole in the seabed at the foot of the Gullet's inner cliff, and that at the lowest tides it can be entered by swimming (area I12);
* warn them of the giant octopus in the flooded cave (area H), which they call "Many-Arms";
* and, if given at least 10 iron knives or comparable gifts, trade **2 pearls of water breathing** — round blue-black pearls, each of which allows one person to breathe water for 2 turns when held in the mouth, after which it dissolves.

If attacked, the mermen will vanish beneath the waves and harass the party's boats for the rest of the adventure (a 1 in 6 chance on each sea trip of a merman ambush — 1d6 mermen trying to overturn the boat).

*12 Mermen warriors (AC 6, HD 1, hp 5 each, #AT 1 trident or spear, D 1d6, MV swim 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 8)*

The mermen's treasure, hidden in a cave beneath the skerry, is 1,000 sp in old coins, 12 pearls (worth 50 gp each), and a silver crown set with coral worth 400 gp. It cannot be reached without magic to breathe water, and taking it will earn the merfolk's undying enmity.

**W6. THE FENNMOUTH AND THE LIZARD MEN (2863-091).** Three leagues north of Kingsreach along the coast, a little beyond the Teeth, the cliffs drop away to sea level and give way to a broad salt marsh, drained by a maze of tidal creeks that the sea fills twice a day. The Fennmouth is a maze of reed beds, mud flats, and winding channels, alive with wading birds, eels, and insects. A rowboat can thread the channels at half speed; a longboat will run aground on a 1–2 on 1d6 each hour.

In the heart of the marsh, on a low mound of mud and reed, is the lair of a tribe of **lizard men**: 8 males, 6 females (who fight as males if the lair is attacked), and 8 young (non-combatants). Their mound is a beehive of mud and wattle, entered through underwater tunnels or a single opening at the top.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
The reeds part on a broad, still pool. In its center rises a great dome of dried mud and woven reed, as big as a cottage, with a ragged hole at the top from which a thread of smoke rises. Bones and broken pots are scattered on the mud around it. On the far bank, three tall, green-scaled figures stand motionless, watching you, spears in their clawed hands.
</div>

The lizard men are not natural allies of the Gullet's monsters; in fact they have been raided by the orcs (area C), who took two of their eggs. Their chief, **Hissk** (*AC 4, HD 3+1, hp 19, #AT 1 club, D 2d4, ML 12*), speaks a few words of the common tongue. If the characters approach without drawing weapons and offer food (they prefer fish or, better, fresh meat), there is a fair chance of a parley (normal reaction roll). The lizard men will give the party nothing for free, but they will trade information for help: if the characters bring back their two stolen eggs from the orc caves (area C6), Hissk will lead the party to the lizard men's own secret way into the Gullet — a narrow sea-cave tunnel, whose seaward mouth lies under the cliffs just outside the Gullet's mouth and can be swum only at low tide; it emerges in the giant octopus's cave (area H). Hissk will guide them there by water. He will also give them his treasure (see below) "as a gift between hunters."

If the party attacks, or if the reaction is hostile, the lizard men fight fiercely and will pursue the party into the water, where they are at their best.

*8 Lizard men (AC 5, HD 2+1, hp 13, 11, 10, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, #AT 1 spear or club, D 1d6+1, MV 60' (20'), swim 120' (40'), Save F2, ML 12)*
*6 Lizard women (as above, hp 9, 8, 8, 7, 6, 5)*

Inside the mound, under a pile of reeds, is the tribe's treasure: 200 ep, 3 gems (worth 50, 100, and 250 gp), an old bronze helmet worth nothing, and a *ring of protection +1* that once belonged to a drowned elf.

**W7. SPIDER COVE (2993-021).** On the far, southern shore of the Sound, three leagues from Kingsreach by boat, a small cove cuts into the cliffs, with a pebble beach where a boat can land at any tide. A steep, slippery gully climbs from the beach to the clifftop, where a dense copse of wind-twisted pines grows in a hollow out of the wind. The copse is the hunting ground of **2 giant crab spiders**, which lurk among the branches, colored exactly like the gray-green pine bark. They surprise their prey on a 1–4 on 1d6.

*2 Giant crab spiders (AC 7, HD 2*, hp 11, 8, #AT 1 bite, D 1d8 + poison (save vs. Poison at +2 or die), MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 7)*

In the center of the copse, wrapped in old webs, are the remains of a party of three adventurers who came to Kingsreach last summer and were never seen again. Their gear has mostly rotted, but among the bones the characters will find: a suit of dwarf-sized chain mail (rusty but usable), a *dagger +1*, a holy symbol of Saint Elmar in silver (worth 25 gp), a pouch with 67 gp and 2 gems (worth 10 gp and 50 gp), and a waterproof scroll-tube holding a *scroll of two spells* (*sleep* and *light*). A diary in the same scroll-tube describes the party's plans to enter the Gullet "by the high path, through the cave of the dog-men" (area G) and ends abruptly.

**W8. THE DROWNED BELL (2863-138).** One league south-southwest of Kingsreach, on the Sound's western shore, a tall stack of rock stands a hundred yards off the cliffs, connected to the beach beneath them only by a reef that is dry at low tide. A path from Kingsreach runs west along the heights (through 2863-126), then turns south and drops down a steep gully to that beach; by boat, it is a league across the head of the Sound. The fisherfolk call the place the Drowned Bell. On its top stand the ruins of a squat stone tower, roofless and long abandoned. This was a shrine of the Drowned Mother in the days before the Realm, built by the wrecker-folk who once lived on this coast. A great bronze bell, green with age, once hung in the tower; it fell long ago and lies half-buried in the rubble.

A boat can land on a ledge at the foot of the stack at any tide but high, and a rough stair carved in the rock climbs 60' to the ruin. The tower's single room, 30' across, is open to the sky. Its walls are carved with worn reliefs of fish, waves, drowning men, and a vast, many-armed female figure rising from the deep. In the center of the floor is a round pit 10' across, choked with rubble, that once led down to the sea.

Lurking in the rubble are **6 skeletons**, the bones of sacrifices, who rise and attack any living thing that enters the tower. They wear rusted chains.

*6 Skeletons (AC 7, HD 1, hp 6, 5, 5, 4, 3, 2, #AT 1, D 1d6, MV 60' (20'), Save F1, ML 12)*

When the skeletons are destroyed, the characters may examine the reliefs. One of them, on the eastern wall, is a carved map: a crescent-shaped cove (the Gullet) in which a spiral of caves descends into the rock, ending in a great carved eye, beside which a fish-shaped hole is cut through the wall to the sea. A character who studies it for a turn will realize that it shows the Drowned Mother's sanctuary deep beneath the Gullet, and that there is a **second way in from the sea** (area I12). Under the fallen bell, if the characters can lift it (combined Strength of 50 or more), lies an ancient offering: a bronze dish holding 48 old gold coins of an unknown stamp (worth 5 gp each to a collector) and a cord strung with three dull blue beads that are in fact *pearls of water breathing* (as those of the merfolk, area W5).

Ringing the bell (if it can be lifted and struck) produces a deep, mournful note that carries for miles. The DM should decide what hears it; the Tidecaller certainly will, and will double the guards at the Drowned Chapel for the next week.

**W9. LIGHTHOUSE ISLE AND THE KINGSLIGHT (2863-149).** Three leagues southeast of Kingsreach, across the mouth of the Sound, lies a long, bare island of heather and gray rock that runs southwest for two leagues (through 2863-154 and 2993-034). On its northern tip, where it looks out over the open sea, stands the lighthouse called the **Kingslight**: a round, whitewashed tower 70' tall. At its top, behind thick glass, burns the great lamp — a basin of whale oil with a dozen wicks, set before a polished brass mirror — which can be seen 20 miles out to sea. A spiral stair climbs the inside wall past three storerooms (oil, wicks, and supplies) to the lamp room.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
A short stone jetty juts from a shingle beach below the tower. Beside it squats a low stone hut, smoke curling from its chimney, and a little skiff painted with a stormy petrel is tied up at the jetty's end. Above, the white tower stands alone against the sky, its great glass eye catching the light. Behind you, across the gray water, the cliffs of Kingsreach look very small and very far away.
</div>

Boats can land at the jetty at any tide. The hut houses the island's guard: a corporal and 2 men-at-arms of the garrison, relieved weekly by boat from the Cauldron. Bored and isolated, they spend more time fishing and dicing than watching, and they trust the lighthouse keepers completely.

<div class="img" markdown="1">
**[ILLUSTRATION — half column]** The lamp room at the top of the Kingslight at night: a great brass mirror behind a blazing basin of wicks, rain streaming down the glass, and a young man with a sly, narrow face reaching for a brass shutter-lever while an old man sleeps in a chair behind him.
</div>

The keeper of the light is **Tobias Fenn** (*AC 9, NM, hp 2, AL L*), a frail, kindly, half-blind old man who has kept the Kingslight for forty years. He sleeps in a small room off the lamp room by day and dozes there by night, leaving the trimming and tending of the lamp more and more to his young assistant.

That assistant is **Corwin Pell** (*AC 7, T2, hp 8, #AT 1 dagger, D 1d4, MV 120' (40'), Save T2, ML 7, AL C; thief abilities as a 2nd-level thief*), a quick, likable young man with a ready laugh, who is secretly in the pay of Black Maudie Strake. On nights when a rich ship is expected and a storm is blowing, Corwin closes the brass shutters on the lamp halfway, dimming the Kingslight to a feeble glow, while Maudie's wreckers burn a false beacon on Hob's Crag above the Gullet. Ships seeking the Sound steer for the false light and run onto the Teeth. Corwin knows which ships are due because the Signal Tower in Kingsreach (area 21) flies their pennants to the lighthouse, and because he reads Ansel Vane's ledgers at the Customs Shed on his weekly supply run to the Cauldron (Ansel has no idea). He sends word to Maudie by a tame gull that roosts in the lamp room, or by sailing out in his skiff, the *Petrel*, which he keeps at the jetty "for fishing."

In Corwin's room in the tower (the second storeroom, where he sleeps among the wick barrels) are hidden: a purse of 85 gp (wrecker's pay, far more than a lamp-boy should have), a silver ring from a drowned merchant (worth 40 gp, engraved "*To Halvard, from his Ingrid*" — the name of the *Merrow*'s captain, which Arn Keel or Dagny would recognize), and a small black stone fish on a cord. Corwin has also used his skill at locks to rob the rented bays in the Rock Warehouses (area 5) twice in the past year, on his supply runs; he keeps a set of thieves' tools under a loose step on the lighthouse stair.

If Corwin is discovered, he will try to bluff, then to bargain (he will gladly betray Maudie to save his own neck), and finally to flee in the *Petrel* for the Gullet. If he is caught and brought to the Castellan, the Castellan will be both grateful and horrified (see area 27).

The rest of the island is empty heather, rock, and seabird colonies. At its far southwestern end (2993-034), a ring of standing stones crowns the highest hill — older than the colony, and left for the DM to develop.

**W10. HILLNEST (2863-101).** Two leagues north of Kingsreach, at the end of the King's Road, lies the colony's only village. The road is no more than a rutted cart track, worn by generations of water-carts; it climbs from the King's Cut over bare, windy heights to the edge of the **downs** — a high, rolling plateau of grass and barley fields that stretches away north for several leagues. Hillnest sits in a fold of the downs around a group of cold springs, the only fresh water in the colony. About two hundred people live here: farmers, shepherds, carters, and their families.

<div class="map" markdown="1">
**[MAP 5 — HILLNEST. A small village plan in blue, 1 square = 20'.]** The King's Road enters from the south and becomes the village's single street, ending at a walled green around the springs. Along the street: the Plough & Fleece alehouse (a), the Reeve's Hall (b), a smithy, and two dozen stone cottages with turf roofs. The Springhouse (c) stands at the head of the green. A windmill (d) stands on a rise to the north, with barley fields beyond. A low drystone wall rings the village, broken by gaps for the road and the sheep-ways. To the west, a line of standing boundary stones (e) marks the edge of the colony's land, beyond which the hills rise toward the Thornwold.
</div>

Hillnest is quieter and friendlier than the fortress. Its people are colonists, born here, and they think of Kingsreach as a noisy, salty place full of soldiers. They know little of the Gullet and care less, but they know every inch of the downs.

**a. THE PLOUGH & FLEECE.** A low, warm alehouse run by **Mags Corrie** (*AC 9, NM, hp 4*), who brews the best ale in the colony and charges half the Salt & Anchor's prices (lodging 1 sp a night in the loft; supper 1 sp). Shepherds drink here in the evenings, and characters may roll for rumors here as at the Salt & Anchor, but the Hillnest folk will tell different stories — about the downs and the Thornwold, not the sea. The DM may add rumors of this sort: "Something has been taking sheep from the high folds — and leaving the fleeces"; "There's a door in the hillside past the boundary stones, and it wasn't there last spring."

**b. THE REEVE'S HALL.** The Castellan's appointed reeve, **Marta Hollin** (*AC 9, NM, hp 5, AL L*), a broad, practical widow, governs the village, collects the grain-tithe, and organizes the water-carts. She keeps a list of every cart that goes down to Kingsreach and every coin it brings back. Marta is no fool: she has noticed that the cult's "pilgrim," Brother Silt, has twice walked up to Hillnest to "pray at the springs," and she did not like the way he looked at the Springhouse. If the characters earn her trust, she will tell them so.

**c. THE SPRINGHOUSE.** A stone house over the largest spring, with a locked door and a sluice that fills the water-carts. Four men of the village militia guard it in turns (*AC 7, NM, hp 4 each, spear, D 1d6, ML 7*). Without the springs, Kingsreach would have only its rain cisterns, and a dry season would empty them; anyone who wished to bring the colony to its knees would start here. (The Tidecaller has thought of this. The DM may use it as a sequel to the Feast, or as a second front on the Feast night itself.)

**d. THE WINDMILL.** **Dunstan Rye** (*AC 9, NM, hp 3*), the miller, grinds the colony's grain. He is a sour, secretive man who pays his tithe late and drinks alone. Whether he is merely unpleasant, or is the "third name" on the Tidecaller's list (area I8), is for the DM to decide.

**e. THE BOUNDARY STONES.** A line of tall standing stones, set by the first colonists, marks the western limit of the colony's land. Beyond it, the hills climb steeply toward three thousand feet, into the trackless briar and rock of the Thornwold. No colonist goes past the stones willingly, and the shepherds bring their flocks in at dusk. The Thornwold is beyond the scope of this module, but it is the obvious next frontier for characters who have cleared the Gullet.
