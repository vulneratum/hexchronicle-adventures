# Concluding the Adventure

The Fortress of Kingsreach does not end when the last cave is cleared. It ends when the Tidecaller's plan is broken — or when it succeeds.

## The Night of the Feast

The Tidecaller has chosen the night of his attack: **the next spring tide that falls during a storm.** The DM should decide when this will be, based on the pace of the campaign. A good choice is about **five to six weeks** after the characters first arrive in Kingsreach, which gives them time to make several expeditions into the Gullet, learn of the plot, and act. If the characters are slow, the DM can simply roll the weather as described under *Sailing* and wait for a storm on a spring tide; if they are quick and thorough, the Feast may never come at all.

**If the characters have destroyed the cult** (slain or driven off the Tidecaller and taken or destroyed the Eye of the Mother), the Feast does not happen. The remaining humanoids of the Gullet, leaderless and frightened, will scatter or fight among themselves. Black Maudie, if still free, will try to flee the coast by boat. The Castellan will reward the characters as described in area 27 and write to the King on their behalf; each character receives a royal letter of commendation and the freedom of Kingsreach — free lodging, free use of the Hoist and the Cauldron, and a seat at the Castellan's table, for life.

**If the characters have exposed Corwin Pell and Brother Silt**, but not destroyed the cult, the Feast will still come, but the Sea Gate will not be opened from within. The Tidecaller's host will break against the chain and the water-gate, and the garrison — warned by the characters — will be waiting. The DM should run the attack as a great battle at the Sea Gate, in which the characters may play a heroic part (defending the Gate Gallery, fighting brine dead climbing the chain, or rowing out in the storm to stop the orc longboat).

**If the characters have done nothing** — or if they have missed the clues — the Feast proceeds as the Tidecaller planned:

1. **That morning**, Corwin Pell sails the *Petrel* across the Sound to the Cauldron on his usual supply run, and does not go back; the storm, he tells Dagny, is too heavy to risk the crossing. **At dusk**, the storm rises. Old Tobias, alone on Lighthouse Isle, cannot trim the great lamp properly by himself, and the Kingslight burns low. Maudie lights the false beacon on Hob's Crag. If any ship is due, it is wrecked on the Teeth.
2. **At midnight**, as the spring tide reaches its height, the brine dead of the Pool of Rising (as many as the Tidecaller has raised — 20 or more by this time) swim out through the Mother's Throat and around the point to the Sea Gate. The orcs' longboat and the wreckers' boats follow, carrying 30 or more wreckers, orcs, goblins, and gnolls.
3. **Corwin Pell and Brother Silt** creep into the Gate Gallery, kill the guards on the capstans with poisoned wine, and lower the chain boom. They then try to raise the water-gate.
4. **The brine dead and the boats enter the Cauldron**, swarm up the Kingstair and the Hoist chains, and attack the fortress. The Tidecaller himself comes last, in the orc longboat, with the Trident of the Deep raised, calling the sea to rise against the walls.

The garrison is not ready. Unless the characters intervene, Kingsreach will fall by dawn. The characters, who will presumably be in the fortress (or may be warned by Dagny, Wren, or Mother Wenna), have one terrible night to stop it. The DM should run this as a series of desperate scenes: a fight in the dark at the capstans to stop Corwin; a rush to wake the Castellan; a stand on the Kingstair against the climbing dead; a duel with the Tidecaller on the quay. If the characters slay the Tidecaller or break his trident, the brine dead lose their purpose and begin to wander aimlessly, and the garrison can mop them up by dawn.

## Further Adventures

Even when the Gullet is cleared, the Reaching Coast holds many more secrets. The DM may wish to develop some of the following:

* **The Thornwold.** What moves in the dark beyond Hillnest's boundary stones? The shepherds lose sheep, the sentries on the Neck Wall see lights on the heights at night, and a door has opened in a hillside that was solid rock last spring. The answer may lie in an old ruin deep in the briars.
* **Hillnest's Springs.** The colony lives on the water of Hillnest. If the cult's last servant in the colony (see area I8) poisons or blocks the Springhouse, Kingsreach has only its cisterns — and a dry season.
* **Lighthouse Isle.** Who raised the ring of standing stones at the island's far end, long before the colony came?
* **The Drowned Mother.** The Tidecaller's notes speak of the Mother "dreaming in the deep beyond the Throat." Is she real? Are there other shrines, other cults, along the coast — perhaps in the free ports to the south?
* **The Third Name.** The Tidecaller's list held a third name in Kingsreach. Who else in the fortress serves the Mother — and are they still waiting?
* **The Merfolk.** The mermen of Seal Skerry may ask for help against some deeper danger — sahuagin, a sea dragon, or a sunken city they dare not enter alone.
* **The Wreck of the *Silver Wren*.** The ghoul bosun's log mentions that the *Silver Wren* carried, besides the pay-chest, "a sealed box for the Castellan from His Majesty's own hand." It was never found. Where is it?
* **The Admiral's War.** Admiral Kittiwake insists that there is "a second Black Fish" far to the north. He may be right.

# New Monsters

**Brine Dead**

Armor Class: 7
Hit Dice: 2
Move: 90' (30'), swim 90' (30')
Attacks: 1 grab
Damage: 1d6 + drowning grip
No. Appearing: 1d4 (2d6)
Save As: Fighter 2
Morale: 12
Treasure Type: Nil
Alignment: Chaotic

Brine dead are the corpses of drowned men and women, raised by evil magic to serve the powers of the deep. They look much as they did in life, but bloated, pale, and gray-green, with weed in their hair and seawater streaming constantly from their mouths and clothes. They move silently, and are as at home underwater as on land.

A brine dead attacks by seizing its victim. If it hits, it does 1d6 damage and holds on: each round thereafter, the victim takes 1d6 damage automatically (as seawater floods from the creature's mouth into his) until he breaks free with a successful Strength check (one try per round, instead of attacking) or the brine dead is destroyed. In water, a held victim cannot breathe and must also check for drowning.

Like all undead, brine dead are unaffected by *sleep*, *charm*, and *hold* spells. They are turned as zombies. They take double damage from fire if they have been out of the water for more than a turn (as they dry out, they become brittle), but are immune to cold. The light of the *Lantern of Saint Elmar* causes them 1d6 damage per round and drives them back.

**Eel, Giant Moray**

Armor Class: 6
Hit Dice: 3
Move: swim 90' (30')
Attacks: 1 bite
Damage: 1d8
No. Appearing: 1 (1d4)
Save As: Fighter 2
Morale: 9
Treasure Type: Nil
Alignment: Neutral

These enormous, mottled eels, up to 15' long, lurk in underwater crevices and wrecks, darting out to seize prey. A giant moray's bite does 1d8 damage, and it will not let go: each round thereafter, it does 1d4 damage automatically, until it is killed, fails a morale check, or the victim breaks free (a Strength check at –4). Giant morays surprise on a 1–4 on 1d6 when lurking in their holes.

**Shellback (Giant Hermit Crab)**

Armor Class: 1 (shell) / 6 (body)
Hit Dice: 4
Move: 60' (20'), swim 30' (10')
Attacks: 2 pincers
Damage: 1d8/1d8
No. Appearing: 1
Save As: Fighter 2
Morale: 8
Treasure Type: Incidental (see area F2)
Alignment: Neutral

The shellback is a hermit crab as big as a pony, which lives inside some large, hollow object — usually the skull of a whale, an old cask, or the bell of a sunken tower. Its "shell" is AC 1, but a character who announces that he is aiming at the soft body, legs, or eyes may attack it at AC 6, at –2 to hit. When reduced to fewer than half its hit points, the shellback withdraws entirely into its shell (AC 1, cannot be harmed except by lifting or breaking the shell) and waits for the danger to pass.

**Sea Lion**

Armor Class: 6
Hit Dice: 3
Move: 30' (10'), swim 180' (60')
Attacks: 1 bite
Damage: 1d8
No. Appearing: 1d6
Save As: Fighter 2

The great sea lions of the northern coasts are fierce, intelligent, and can be trained by a patient master (as Admiral Kittiwake has trained Duchess, area W4). A sea lion in the water may attempt to overturn a small boat, succeeding on a 1–3 on 1d6 (skiffs) or 1 on 1d6 (rowboats).

# New Magic Items

**Lantern of Saint Elmar.** This plain brass ship's lantern, with a small silver star soldered to its top, belongs to the Chapel of Saint Elmar (area 19). When lit by a lawful character, it burns without fuel and sheds a clear, golden light in a 30' radius. Undead within the light suffer 1d6 damage per round (no save), and must make a morale check each round or flee from it; brine dead suffer double damage. Further, any false or magical light — including the wreckers' beacon on Hob's Crag, seen from the sea — appears as a sickly green when viewed through the lantern's glass. In the hands of a chaotic character, the lantern goes dark.

**Trident of the Deep.** This black iron trident, its prongs shaped like twisting fish, is a *trident +1*. Once per day, the wielder may strike it on the ground and call a wave of seawater from any body of water within 30' to sweep a path 10' wide and 30' long: all creatures in the path must save vs. Death Ray or be knocked down and dragged 2d10' toward the water, taking 1d6 damage (and possibly going into the sea). In the hands of a chaotic cleric, it also allows the wielder to control up to 2 HD of undead per level, as long as they are within 120'. Any lawful character who holds it feels the cold of the deep sea, and takes 1 point of damage per round.

**Pearls of Water Breathing.** These round, blue-black pearls are made by the merfolk from a secret art. A creature who holds one in his mouth may breathe water as if it were air for 2 turns, after which the pearl dissolves. While breathing water, the character cannot speak (and so cannot cast spells).

**Whistle of the Winds.** This silver bosun's whistle, once blown, calls a steady breeze in whatever direction the blower faces, which lasts for 1 hour. A sailing boat in this wind moves at full speed regardless of the weather; a rowboat may ship its oars and drift. The whistle can be used once per day. If blown three times in a single day, it calls a storm (DM's discretion) and cracks, becoming useless.

**Ring of Water Walking.** As described in the standard rules: the wearer may walk on the surface of water as though it were solid ground.

## The Fortress of Kingsreach

Across the sea from the Realm, on the cliffs of a lonely colony that no road can reach, stands the fortress of Kingsreach. Ships slip in through a tunnel at the waterline, past a great chain and an iron-shod gate, into a hidden harbor at the bottom of a cliff-walled well. Above them, on the clifftop, the King's banner snaps in the wind, and across the bay the Kingslight burns on its lonely isle to guide sailors past the reef called the Teeth.

But ships are dying on the Teeth, and a false light burns on the heights above the Gullet. A league along the coast, in that narrow cove, monsters have gathered in the caves — and in the deepest cave of all, where the sea comes and goes, something is calling the drowned to rise.

*The Fortress of Kingsreach* is an introductory adventure for 4–9 characters of levels 1–3, for use with any Basic-era fantasy role-playing game. It includes a fully keyed fortress and its farming village, a coast of islets, reefs, and wrecks to explore by boat, with every site keyed to its Hex Chronicle address, rules for sailing, swimming, and the tides, sixty keyed cave areas on three tiers of the Gullet's cliffs, new monsters and magic items, pre-rolled characters, and a climactic night of storm and siege.
