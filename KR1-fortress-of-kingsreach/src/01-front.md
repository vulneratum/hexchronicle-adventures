This module contains a complete description booklet and the keys to five maps, forming a ready-made scenario for any Basic-era fantasy role-playing game (Old-School Essentials, B/X, BECMI, and their many compatible cousins). It has been designed so that a Dungeon Master new to the craft may begin play with a minimum of preparation, while still offering the seasoned referee a living base of operations, a dangerous coastline, and a warren of sea caves that changes with every turn of the tide.

Within are many features to aid novice players and Dungeon Masters alike: legends, a table of rumors, rules for boats and the tides, a fortress full of townsfolk to bargain with (and one or two to be wary of), and the Caves of the Gullet — a cliff of monster lairs that may be reached only at the mercy of the sea.

# Introduction

Welcome to the land of imagination! You are about to begin a voyage to a lonely colony at the edge of the known world, where the sea is the only road home and every cave mouth in the cliffs may hide a monster's lair. If you plan to participate in this adventure as a **player**, stop reading now. The information in the rest of this module is for your Dungeon Master (DM), so that he or she may guide you through the adventure. Knowing too much about what lies ahead will spoil the surprises and the fun.

If you are the **Dungeon Master**, read this entire module carefully before play begins, so that you are familiar with the fortress, the coast, and the caves, and with the tides that govern them all. Then make notes as needed — mark the hit points of monsters as they are wounded, note which caves have been cleared, and track the hour of the day so that you know whether the tide is high or low.

This module is divided into several parts:

**THE FORTRESS OF KINGSREACH** is the player characters' home base: a royal stronghold on the cliffs of a far colony, whose harbor can be entered only through a fortified sea-tunnel. Here they may buy equipment, hire men-at-arms and boats, rest, heal, gather rumors, and perhaps uncover a traitor.

**THE REACHING COAST** is the wilderness surrounding the fortress — a stretch of sea cliff, salt marsh, islets, and reefs that the characters will explore by boat and on foot — together with Hillnest, the colony's farming village at the end of the King's Road.

**THE CAVES OF THE GULLET** are the heart of the adventure: a narrow, cliff-walled cove one league (three miles) east of Kingsreach, its walls honeycombed with sea caves on three levels. Monsters of many kinds dwell there — some at war with one another — and the lowest caves can only be entered when the tide is out.

**THE APPENDICES** include new monsters and magic items, a roster of pre-rolled characters, and hirelings available in the fortress.

## Notes for the Dungeon Master

The Fortress of Kingsreach is meant to be played over several sessions. The player characters arrive by ship, establish themselves in the fortress, and then make expeditions — first along the coast, and eventually into the Gullet. They return to Kingsreach between forays to heal, spend their gold, and resupply. Kingsreach is a colony, and its only link with the wider world is the sea. Close to home, a cliff path leads to the Gullet and a rutted cart track climbs to Hillnest, but most of the coast's sites — and the Gullet's lowest caves — are best reached by boat, and the DM should make this feel real: the boat must be hired or bought, rowed or sailed, beached or anchored somewhere safe, and watched while the party is ashore. A party that loses its boat may be stranded!

**Party Size.** The adventure is designed for a party of six to nine characters of 1st level, or four to six characters of 2nd or 3rd level. A smaller party should be encouraged to hire men-at-arms or marines in the fortress (see the Hirelings appendix). If you have only one or two players, let each run two characters, and give the party a non-player character or two from the roster in the back of this booklet.

**Equipment.** Beginning characters may purchase any normal equipment from the fortress at the prices listed in your rule book, *except* that the items listed on the *Kingsreach Price List* (see the Fortress of Kingsreach) are scarcer or more common here than elsewhere. In addition, the DM may grant each character a small family heirloom — a *potion of healing*, a dagger +1, or three silver-tipped arrows — to help them find fortune before fortune finds them.

**Intelligent Monsters.** The monsters of the Gullet are not mere obstacles waiting in rooms. Many of them are intelligent, and they will react to the party's raids. If the characters attack the goblins and withdraw, the goblins will post extra guards, set traps, or send for help from their allies. If the characters slay the kobold chief, the kobolds may flee to another cave. Use common sense: monsters warned of an attack will not sit idle. Equally, monsters will not cooperate with every other monster — the orcs and goblins despise one another, and the cult of the Drowned Mother is feared by all. Clever players can turn these rivalries to their advantage.

**The Sea Is Not a Road.** Swimming in armor is very dangerous (see *Sailing, Swimming, and the Tides*), boats may be swamped, and the tide will trap the careless. Warn players fairly the first time they court disaster — an old salt in the fortress is the perfect mouthpiece — but thereafter let the waves fall where they may.

**Death and Survival.** The DM should give the player characters a fair chance. If the players are rash, they will lose characters. Dead characters cannot be raised in Kingsreach (the Curate is not powerful enough), although a body might be carried home across the sea to the Realm's capital at great expense — 1,000 gp or more. New characters can always arrive on the next supply ship.

## Background

Across the eastern sea lies the Realm, an old kingdom that has long since run out of room. Eighty years ago, King Aldous the Builder sent ships west to plant a colony on the far shore — and on a line of high, cliff-girt hills above a deep bay, his captains raised the fortress-town of **Kingsreach**.

No road links the colony to the rest of the continent. Behind its narrow coastal strip, the land climbs into the **Thornwold**, a trackless upland of briar, rock, and wind where no road has ever survived a single winter and where things older than the Realm still walk. The colony is a pocket of the Realm set down on a foreign shore, and the sea is its only road home.

The colony is small. Kingsreach itself stands on high cliffs above a deep bay called **the Sound**, where the pearl beds lie. An unpaved cart track, the **King's Road**, climbs two leagues north to **Hillnest**, a farming village on the high downs that grows the colony's grain and fills its water-carts. Across the mouth of the Sound, on a rocky isle, the lighthouse called **the Kingslight** guards the approach and the deadly reef called **the Teeth**. Beyond those few miles, the colony ends.

King Aldous's engineers built Kingsreach to hold against anything. On its landward side they cut a great ditch sixty feet deep — **the King's Cut** — crossed only by a drawbridge on the King's Road. On its seaward side, the sea had long ago carved a great cavern in the cliffs that later collapsed, leaving a deep, round, open-topped basin — a natural harbor joined to the sea by a single tunnel. The engineers widened the tunnel, closed it with a water-gate and a chain boom, and cut a stair and a great hoist up the basin's walls to the fortress above. Ships pass through the **Sea Gate** into the sheltered water of the **Cauldron**, and their cargoes rise to the walls by winch and treadwheel. No enemy ship can enter the harbor unless the garrison permits it.

For two generations the colony prospered in a modest way. Pearl divers, fishing smacks, and the King's supply ship came and went; Hillnest's carts rolled down the King's Road; the Kingslight burned every night. But in the last few years, things have changed for the worse:

**Ships are being lost on the Teeth.** Three merchantmen have broken up on the reef in the past two years, on nights when the Kingslight was surely burning. Their cargoes were never recovered, and few of their crews were ever seen again. Sailors whisper of a second light seen on the heights above the Gullet on those nights — a false beacon.

**Monsters have come to the Gullet.** One league east of Kingsreach is a narrow, steep-walled cove called the Gullet, its cliffs riddled with sea caves. For years only seals and smugglers used it. Now its caves teem with humanoids — kobolds, goblins, orcs, and worse — who have taken to raiding fishing boats, stealing nets, and once even attacking a pearl-diving party in broad daylight. No one knows why they have gathered there, but old fishermen say the drowned have been seen walking on the Gullet's beach at the lowest tides.

**The garrison is too small.** Kingsreach's Castellan has only enough soldiers to hold the walls, the Sea Gate, and the Kingslight. He cannot spare men to clear the Gullet. His petitions across the sea for reinforcements have gone unanswered — or perhaps, he has begun to suspect, have never arrived at all.

And so the Castellan has done what lords of the borderlands have always done: he has let it be known, in every port where the King's supply ship calls, that **brave adventurers are welcome at Kingsreach**, and that whatever they take from the Gullet, they may keep.

### What Is Really Going On (DM Only)

Beneath the Gullet lies a half-drowned shrine to a dark sea power called **the Drowned Mother** — an ancient, hungry thing worshipped by the wrecker-folk of this coast long before the Realm's ships came. A renegade priest named **Sedge Harrow, the Tidecaller,** rediscovered the shrine four years ago. With his small band of acolytes, he has drawn monsters to the Gullet with promises of plunder and protection, and he raises the drowned dead to serve as his guards.

The Tidecaller's partner in crime is **Black Maudie Strake**, captain of a band of human wreckers who dwell in the Gullet's upper caves. Maudie's people light false beacons on the cliffs on stormy nights, luring ships onto the Teeth; the cult takes the drowned sailors for its rites, and the wreckers and their humanoid allies take the cargo. To know when rich ships are due, Maudie has a spy in the King's own service: **Corwin Pell**, the young assistant keeper of the Kingslight on Lighthouse Isle, who also dims the true beacon on the nights a wreck is planned. Corwin learns which ships are due on his supply runs to Kingsreach, and his messages go out by carrier gull and by a small boat that "goes fishing" at night.

The Tidecaller's ultimate goal is far more ambitious than wrecking. He means to raise a great host of the drowned dead on the night of the next spring-tide storm, and with Maudie's help to open the Sea Gate of Kingsreach from within. If the fortress falls, the Drowned Mother will have her feast, and the Tidecaller will have a fortress of his own.

None of this need ever be told outright to the players. They will learn it piece by piece, from rumors, captured letters, prisoners, and what they find in the caves. If they never learn it — well, the spring-tide storm will come in its own time (see *Concluding the Adventure*).

## Start

When you are ready to begin, read the following boxed text aloud to the players. Then let them describe what their characters do.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
You are members of an ancient and honorable calling — adventurers — and like all adventurers you have heard the call of the frontier. In the taverns of the port city, a weatherbeaten sea captain spoke of the Realm's lonely colony on the far shore of the western sea, where the King's own Castellan offers welcome to any who will brave the monster-haunted caves along its coast. "No road goes there," she said. "Only my ship. Pay your passage and pull a rope when I tell you, and I'll set you down in the safest harbor in the world."

Nine days later you have paid, and pulled many ropes. Now the *Gannet* is running before a cold east wind toward a wall of dark cliffs that rises sheer from a white collar of surf. High atop them squats a gray fortress. Off to the south, across the mouth of a deep bay, a white lighthouse stands on a rocky isle; to the north, a long white line of breakers marks a reef. You can see no beach, no road, no landing of any kind — only the cliffs, and the gulls.

Then the captain puts her helm over, and you see it: an archway at the foot of the cliff, just above the waterline, with a great chain hanging across it like a tooth-gapped smile. A horn sounds from somewhere high above. Slowly, link by link, the chain sinks beneath the water, and the *Gannet* glides into the dark.

# Sailing, Swimming, and the Tides

Because the colony's only link with the world is the sea, and most of its coast is best reached by boat, the DM will need a few simple rules for boats, the water, and the tide. These rules are intentionally brief; the DM should feel free to use more detailed rules from an expert rule book if desired.

Characters rowing their own boat should have at least one person who knows how (a fighter, dwarf, or halfling raised on the coast, a hired sailor, or any character who succeeds on a Dexterity check the first time out). Otherwise, the boat makes only half speed, and the DM should check for swamping whenever the sea is rough.

**Weather.** At the start of each day, roll 1d6 for the sea state: **1–3** calm; **4–5** choppy (rowing speed –¼, small boats check for swamping once per hour); **6** rough (rowing speed halved, check for swamping every turn; no fishing smack will put out). If the DM rolls 6 two days in a row, a storm blows in that lasts 1d3 days, during which no boat may safely leave the Cauldron. Storms are the wreckers' favorite weather.

**Swamping.** A swamped boat fills with water and capsizes. Everyone aboard is in the water (see Swimming). A swamped rowboat or skiff can be righted and bailed by its crew in 1d4 turns if they can reach it and the sea calms; otherwise it is lost.

**Watching the Boat.** A boat left beached in the Gullet while the party is in the caves will be noticed by the monsters. For each hour it is left unguarded, there is a 1 in 6 chance it is stolen, holed, or set adrift (by goblins, orcs, or the tide itself). A boat drawn up above the high-water mark on the North Beach is safe from the tide, but not from the goblins.

## Swimming

Any character not wearing armor may swim at half normal movement. A character in leather armor may swim at one-quarter movement, but must make a Strength check each round or go under. A character in metal armor **cannot swim** and will sink, drowning unless rescued or freed of the armor (removing chain or plate in the water takes 1d4 rounds and a successful Dexterity check each round). A character who is underwater may hold his breath for a number of rounds equal to one-third of his Constitution score; after that he must save vs. Death Ray each round or drown. Characters carrying more than 400 coins of weight sink as if in metal armor.

## The Tides

The tide is the most important thing in this adventure after the monsters themselves. The sea along the Reaching Coast rises and falls some fifteen feet twice each day, and much of the Gullet is submerged at high water. The DM should keep track of the hour of the day, using the simple tide cycle below. For convenience, assume that **low tide falls at dawn and again at dusk** on the first day the characters arrive, and that it comes one hour later each following day.

| Tide Phase | Duration | Effects in the Gullet |
|---|---|---|
| **Low Tide** | 2 hours | All beaches exposed. Tide Tier caves (A, F, H) open and walkable. The Sunken Stair (area I1) is dry. |
| **Rising** | 4 hours | Beaches shrink. Tide Tier entrances knee-deep, then waist-deep. Rowing into the Gullet is easy (the current flows in). |
| **High Tide** | 2 hours | Gullet beaches gone. Tide Tier caves flooded to the ceiling at their mouths — enter only by swimming 30' underwater. Boats can reach the Shelf Tier directly. |
| **Falling** | 4 hours | As Rising, but rowing *out* of the Gullet is easy and rowing *in* is slow (half speed). |

The DM should remind the players of the tide when their characters can see it. Natives of Kingsreach can tell the tide at a glance, and a tide-table is painted on the wall of the Harbormaster's office (area 3). Characters who lose track of time underground will have no idea what the sea is doing — which is exactly when the sea is most dangerous.

**Spring Tides.** Twice each month, at the new and full moons, the tides run higher and lower than usual. At spring low tide, the deepest parts of the Gullet are exposed (see area I12). At spring high tide, even the Shelf Tier floods to the knee. The Tidecaller's great ritual (see *Concluding the Adventure*) is planned for a spring tide that coincides with a storm.

## Encounters at Sea

When the party is on the water outside the Cauldron, the DM should check for a wandering encounter once every two hours (or once each time the party crosses the open water between two places on the sea chart), rolling 1d6. An encounter occurs on a roll of 1. Then roll 1d10 on the table below.

| d10 | Encounter at Sea |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Fishing smack** from Kingsreach (crew of 3 NM). Friendly; will share news and one rumor. |

| 3 | **Giant crabs** (1d2) clinging to a rock the boat must pass; they lunge at anyone within 5'. |
