# The Fortress of Kingsreach

<div class="map" markdown="1">
**[MAP 2 — THE FORTRESS OF KINGSREACH (Hex Chronicle 2863-127). Two-part map, printed in the classic pale-blue-on-white style with a 20-foot grid on the fortress portion. North is at the top; the fortress lies on a promontory pointing southeast.]**

**Upper inset (cross-section, left margin):** A vertical slice through the promontory showing the sea at the bottom, the Sea Gate tunnel cut at the waterline, the round harbor basin (the Cauldron) rising as a shaft open to the sky, the zigzag Kingstair climbing its southwest wall, the Hoist (a treadwheel crane) at the basin's lip, and the fortress walls on the clifftop 150' above the water.

**Lower level — the Cauldron (scale 1 square = 20'):** A roughly circular basin of water about 400' across. The Sea Gate tunnel (area 1) enters from the southeast, 200' long and 40' wide, with the chain boom marked at its outer mouth and the iron water-gate at its inner end; a narrow Gate Gallery with arrow slits runs above the tunnel along its northeast side. A stone quay (area 4) curves along the southwest and northwest rim of the basin. On the quay, starting beside the tunnel and working around: the Harbormaster's House (3), the Customs Shed (4), three Rock Warehouses cut into the cliff (5), Arn Keel's Boatyard with a slipway (6), and the foot of the Hoist (8). The Fisherfolk's Landing (7) is a cluster of huts on rock ledges on the northeast rim, reached by a plank walkway. The Kingstair (9) begins beside the Harbormaster's House and switchbacks up the southwest wall, with the Halfway Post marked at the midpoint.

**Upper level — the clifftop fortress (scale 1 square = 20'):** The fortress fills the promontory, about 820' from its landward (northwest) end to its seaward (southeast) tip and 560' across. The Cauldron's rim, 400' across, is a fenced, railed hole in the seaward half of the Outer Bailey (11), with the Hoist's crane-house (8) and the Head Gate (10) beside it. Along the southwest wall, overlooking the Sound: the Cistern House (12), the Salt & Anchor (13), the Smithy (14), the Provisioner (15), the Chandlery (16), and the Factor's House (17). Along the northeast wall, facing the open sea: the Mariners' Guild Hall (18), the Chapel of Saint Elmar (19), the Barracks (20), and the Fishmarket (24). At the southeast tip, on its own walled spur, stands the Signal Tower (21). Four Watch Towers (22) stand at the corners of the curtain wall. At the landward (northwest) end, the Neck Wall and Landgate (23) rise above the sixty-foot King's Cut, crossed by a drawbridge that carries the King's Road away north toward Hillnest. Inside the Neck Wall, a second wall encloses the Inner Ward: the Inner Gate (25), the Great Hall (26), the Castellan's Tower (27), the Armory (28), and the Gaol (29). Ballista platforms (30) are marked on the northeast and southwest sea walls.

Kingsreach is a royal fortress-town, and its people are soldiers, sailors, fisherfolk, and the handful of merchants who serve them. Some six hundred souls live within its walls, counting the garrison, and two hundred more in Hillnest up the King's Road. Everyone in the colony knows everyone else, and strangers are noticed — welcomed, generally, for the Castellan has ordered it, but noticed.

**General Notes.** Kingsreach is built of dark gray basalt, quarried from the Cauldron when the harbor was widened. Its walls are 30' high and 10' thick, with a walkway along the top protected by battlements. All buildings within the walls are stone with slate roofs, built low against the wind. There are no trees within the fortress, and no fresh springs; all water comes from rain cisterns and from the water-carts that roll down the King's Road from Hillnest's springs, and **drinking water is rationed** (see area 12). Firewood is precious, brought in by ship or by cart from the downs; peat, dried kelp, and seal oil are burned instead, and the fortress smells of all three.

**The Law.** The Castellan's word is law. Characters who brawl, steal, or draw weapons within the walls will be arrested by the watch (4 men-at-arms and a corporal, arriving within 1d4 rounds of a disturbance in the bailey) and brought before the Castellan, who may fine them, imprison them in the Gaol, or — for serious crimes — put them on the next ship out, never to return. Murder is punished by hanging from the Gallows Arm over the sea wall. Characters who behave themselves will find the folk of Kingsreach honest, tight-lipped, and grateful for any help against the Gullet.

**Treasure and Taxes.** The Castellan claims no share of any treasure taken from the Gullet or the coast. Goods *sold* in the fortress pay a tax of 1 sp in every 10 gp to the Factor (area 17), which the merchants collect and the characters will scarcely notice. The Castellan does, however, insist on one thing: **any letters, maps, or papers** found in the Gullet must be shown to him, and he will pay 10 gp or more for any that prove useful.

**Day and Night.** The Head Gate (area 10) is closed at sunset and opened at dawn, and the Landgate's drawbridge (area 23) is raised at sunset and lowered at dawn. The Sea Gate is closed at sunset and opened at dawn, and will not be opened at night for anyone except by the Castellan's own order. Characters who return from the Gullet by boat after dark will have to wait outside — in their boat, in the swell, at the foot of the cliff — until morning (those who come back by the cliff path must wait at the King's Cut), unless they can persuade the Gate Sergeant otherwise (a successful reaction roll at –2, or a bribe of at least 10 gp, which the sergeant will refuse if anyone else is watching).

**The Garrison.** The Castellan commands 60 soldiers, organized as follows:

* 36 men-at-arms (chain mail and shield, sword or spear; *AC 4, F1, hp 6 each, #AT 1, D 1d8, MV 90' (30'), Save F1, ML 9*) in three watches of 12.
* 12 crossbowmen (leather armor, crossbow, short sword; *AC 7, F1, hp 5 each, #AT 1, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 8*).
* 6 marines (chain mail, cutlass, and hand-axe; trained to fight on ships and in boats; *AC 5, F2, hp 11 each, #AT 1, D 1d8, MV 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 10*).
* 3 corporals (*AC 4, F2, hp 12 each, D 1d8, ML 10*), 2 sergeants (*AC 3, F3, hp 18 each, D 1d8, ML 10*), and the Captain of the Watch (area 20).

At any time, one third of the garrison is on duty, posted at the Sea Gate, the Head Gate, the Landgate, the towers, and the Kingslight (a corporal and 2 men-at-arms on Lighthouse Isle, relieved weekly); the rest are in the Barracks, eating, sleeping, or drilling.

## Key to the Fortress of Kingsreach

### The Sea Gate and the Cauldron (Lower Level)

**1. THE SEA GATE.** The only entrance to Kingsreach is a tunnel 200' long, 40' wide, and 30' high, cut through the living rock at the waterline. The tunnel holds 15' of water even at the lowest tide. At its outer mouth, a **chain boom** of iron links as thick as a man's arm can be raised or lowered by two great capstans in the Gate Gallery above; when raised, it hangs a foot above the waterline and no vessel larger than a coracle can pass beneath it. At the tunnel's inner end, a massive **water-gate** of iron-banded oak can be lowered like a portcullis to close the passage completely.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
The tunnel is cold and dripping, lit at intervals by lanterns hung on iron brackets. The sound of the sea booms and echoes off the vaulted roof. High on the right-hand wall, a row of narrow slits looks down on you, and here and there you glimpse a helmet or the end of a crossbow. Ahead, a great dark gate hangs above the water, its iron-shod teeth dripping. Beyond it lies daylight.
</div>

Above the tunnel, along its north side, runs the **Gate Gallery**, a long rock-cut chamber with twelve arrow slits overlooking the water and six murder holes in the tunnel roof. The capstans for the chain and the winch for the water-gate are here. The Gallery is always manned by 6 crossbowmen, 4 men-at-arms, and the **Gate Sergeant, Bertrand Cole** (*AC 3, F3, hp 18, #AT 1, D 1d8, ML 10, AL L*), a thick-necked, humorless veteran who knows every boat that uses the Sea Gate and distrusts those he does not. Cauldrons of oil and baskets of rocks stand ready by the murder holes. The Gallery connects to the Harbormaster's House (area 3) by a stair.

Every vessel that enters is hailed from the Gallery and must state its name and business. Boats belonging to adventurers will be recognized after their first few trips. If a boat comes in at speed, or with a crew the sergeant doesn't know, he will have the crossbows trained on it and the water-gate half-lowered until he is satisfied.

**2. THE CAULDRON.** The harbor of Kingsreach is a round basin of deep, still, green water about 400' across, ringed by sheer cliffs 150' high and open to the sky. Even when storms roar outside, the Cauldron is calm; only a gentle surge marks the turning of the tide. A dozen fishing smacks, rowboats, and coracles are usually moored here, along with the King's supply ship the *Gannet* when she is in port (once every three weeks, staying two days). Seabirds nest on every ledge, and the cliffs are streaked white with their droppings.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
You emerge from the tunnel into a vast well of green water, like the bottom of a giant's cup. Cliffs rise on every side to a ragged circle of sky far above, where gulls wheel and cry. A stone quay curves around the southern and eastern edges of the pool, crowded with sheds, stacks of barrels, nets hung to dry, and busy people. A long stair zigzags up the cliff face, and at the top of the cliff a great timber crane leans out over the edge, lowering a platform on chains.
</div>

**3. HARBORMASTER'S HOUSE.** This stout stone house at the seaward end of the quay, beside the tunnel, is the office and home of the harbormaster, **Dagny Oarsdotter** (*AC 9, F3, hp 15, #AT 1 belaying pin, D 1d4, ML 9, AL L*), a broad, red-faced woman of fifty with a voice that can carry across the Cauldron in a gale. Dagny was a whaler before she came here; she knows the Reaching Coast better than anyone except perhaps the pearl divers, and she has no patience for fools. Every new arrival must pay her a **landing fee of 1 gp per person**, and every boat that uses the Cauldron must be registered with her.

A large tide-table is painted on her wall, and she will tell anyone the hour of tomorrow's low water for free. For a little flattery (or 5 gp, which she will put in the Widows' Box for drowned sailors' families), she will share one rumor and give sound advice about the Gullet: "Go in on the falling tide, come out on the rising, and never leave your boat on the beach without a guard." If the characters later prove themselves against the Gullet, Dagny becomes a staunch friend, and will warn them if she notices anything strange — for example, that Corwin Pell's little boat has been out fishing on nights when no sane fisherman would go.

Dagny keeps 45 gp in fees in a locked strongbox under her bed, and a pair of loaded crossbows behind her door.

**4. THE QUAY AND CUSTOMS SHED.** The quay is a wide stone ledge running around the southwest and northwest sides of the Cauldron, 6' above the high-water mark, lined with iron mooring rings. It is always busy by day: fishermen unloading, dockhands rolling barrels, children fishing with lines, gulls fighting over scraps. The Customs Shed is a long open-fronted building where incoming cargo is inspected and counted by **Ansel Vane** (*AC 9, NM, hp 3*), the Factor's clerk, a nervous, ink-stained young man who is entirely honest and entirely terrified of the Castellan. Characters bringing goods to sell will have them listed here. Ansel has nothing of value but his ledgers.

**5. ROCK WAREHOUSES.** Three deep storerooms have been cut into the cliff behind the quay, each closed with an iron-bound door and a heavy lock. They hold the fortress's supplies: grain, salt meat, oil, timber, arrows and crossbow bolts, and cloth. Each is guarded by one man-at-arms by day and two at night. Characters may rent a small locked bay in the third warehouse for 1 gp per week, which is the safest place in Kingsreach to keep goods — though not, as it happens, perfectly safe (see area W9).

**6. ARN KEEL'S BOATYARD.** A slipway, a cluttered workshop, and a sail-loft belong to **Arn Keel** (*AC 9, NM, hp 4, AL N*), the fortress shipwright, an ancient, toothless, and surprisingly cheerful man who has built or mended every boat in the Cauldron. Arn will sell or hire boats at the prices listed under *Boats*. He has on hand, at any time, 2 skiffs, 2 rowboats, and 1 longboat (the *Cormorant*, rather old; it leaks, and must be bailed for one turn in every hour). Arn will repair a holed boat for 10% of its price, taking 1d3 days.

If the characters show Arn a broken bit of wreckage marked with a ship's name, he can tell them which ship it came from and when it was lost. He is very fond of the dead crews and will give the characters a free week's boat hire for proof of who wrecked them.

**7. FISHERFOLK'S LANDING.** On the northeast side of the Cauldron, a rickety plank walkway leads to a cluster of driftwood huts on rock ledges, where the fortress's fisherfolk and pearl divers live — about forty people, mostly families. They are a close, superstitious lot: there are bundles of seaweed and gull feathers tied over every door "against the drowned." They do not much like soldiers or merchants, but they will warm to adventurers who share their catch or their luck.

The most useful person here is **Wren Tallow** (*AC 8, T1, hp 4, #AT 1 knife, D 1d4, MV 120' (40'), swim 90', ML 7, AL N*), a sharp-eyed girl of seventeen who is the best pearl diver in Kingsreach. Wren's older brother Pell Tallow was lost when the goblins of the Gullet attacked a diving party last spring, and she has a fierce grudge against the Gullet's monsters. For 2 gp a day and a share of the treasure, she will guide the party into the Gullet. She knows the tide-paths, the location of the Tide Tier caves (A, F, and H), the beach where boats can be drawn up, and the rough layout of the goblin caves (B), but she will not willingly go near the Drowned Chapel (area I). Wren can swim like a seal, and if the party's boat is threatened, she will try to guard it.

The fishermen will also tell anyone who asks about the "lost lights" — on stormy nights, some of them have seen a second beacon burning on the heights above the Gullet, a league to the east. "And the Kingslight looked dim those nights, too," one old woman will add, "but nobody listens to me."

**8. THE HOIST.** A great timber crane on the clifftop lowers a railed wooden platform 20' square on four chains to the quay below. The crane is powered by a huge treadwheel in a shed on the clifftop, walked by two burly laborers. A ride up or down takes 3 turns and costs 1 sp per person or per 1,000 coins of cargo; the Hoist can carry up to 12 people or 20,000 coins of weight at a time. It is the only way to move heavy goods to the fortress above. The Hoist does not run at night, and is chained and locked at the top when not in use.

**9. THE KINGSTAIR.** A narrow stair of 312 steps zigzags up the south wall of the Cauldron from the quay to the Head Gate. It has a stone parapet on the outer side, but the steps are worn and slippery, and climbing in armor is exhausting: anyone climbing in metal armor arrives winded (–1 to hit for one turn). The climb takes 2 turns. Halfway up, at a broad landing, is the **Halfway Post**, a small guardhouse built into the rock with 2 men-at-arms on duty, who can drop a heavy oak gate across the stair. The Kingstair is open to anyone by day and closed at night.

### The Clifftop Fortress (Upper Level)

**10. THE HEAD GATE.** The top of the Kingstair and the crane-house of the Hoist both open into a small walled yard called the Head Gate. Two squat towers flank an iron portcullis leading into the Outer Bailey. The yard is overlooked by battlements on all sides, so that anyone who forces his way up from the Cauldron must fight through a killing ground. The Head Gate is held by 4 men-at-arms and a corporal at all times, with 4 crossbowmen on the towers.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
At the top of the stair, a wind straight off the ocean nearly knocks you from your feet. You stand in a small stone yard between two squat gray towers. Through an open portcullis you see a wide, windswept courtyard, slate roofs, smoke whipped sideways from chimneys, and beyond them, at the seaward tip of the walls, a slim stone tower flying colored flags. Far out across the bay, a white lighthouse stands on a rocky isle. A guard in a dented kettle helm looks you up and down and asks your names and business.
</div>

New arrivals are asked their names and business, and are told the three rules of the fortress: no fighting, no stealing, and no wasting water. Characters who give a good account of themselves are directed to the Salt & Anchor (area 13) and told that the Castellan will "send for you when he has a mind to."

**11. THE OUTER BAILEY.** The wide courtyard of Kingsreach is paved with flat stones, swept constantly by the sea wind. In its seaward half lies the railed rim of the Cauldron, a dizzying drop of 150' to the water. There is always something happening in the bailey by day: soldiers drilling, children chasing gulls, fishwives arguing, nets being mended, goats grazing on the scrubby grass along the walls. A tall pole near the Head Gate flies the Royal banner, and a smaller pole beside it flies colored pennants to signal the tide and the weather.

**12. THE CISTERN HOUSE.** This low, thick-walled building covers four great rain cisterns cut into the rock, fed by channels from every roof in the fortress. It is the most closely guarded building in Kingsreach after the Castellan's Tower: 2 men-at-arms stand at its door day and night. Each resident of Kingsreach is entitled to a ration of two skins of water a day, drawn at dawn; visitors must buy theirs, at 1 sp a skin, from the **Water Warden, Hob Nettle** (*AC 9, NM, hp 3*), a dour little man who keeps a tally of every drop. Anyone caught stealing water is flogged. In a dry season, the ration is halved and tempers run short. (A clever DM may use the threat of a poisoned cistern as a later adventure hook.)

**13. THE SALT & ANCHOR.** This long, low inn is the social heart of Kingsreach. Its common room is warm, smoky, and loud, hung with ships' lanterns, whale ribs, carved figureheads, and the flags of lost ships. The inn is run by **Brannoc Hake** and his wife **Elsa** (both *AC 9, NM, hp 4*), a cheerful, gossipy couple who have seen many adventurers come and go — and fewer come back.

<div class="img" markdown="1">
**[ILLUSTRATION — quarter page]** The common room of the Salt & Anchor: a low-beamed tavern lit by swinging ship's lanterns, a carved mermaid figurehead mounted over the hearth, a stout innkeeper drawing ale, and a table of rough sailors arm-wrestling while a hooded figure watches from a corner booth.

The inn has a common sleeping loft (2 sp per night), six small private rooms (1 gp per night), and one fine room at the top with a window over the sea (5 gp per night). Meals cost 3 sp (fish stew and black bread) or 1 gp (roast goat, when there is any). Ale is 1 sp a mug; the fortress's own fiery seaweed spirit, called *wrack*, is 5 cp a cup and is said to be good for the lungs.

On any evening, the common room holds 2d6 off-duty soldiers, 1d6 fishermen, 1d4 sailors from whatever ship is in port, and one or two of the more interesting folk listed below. Rumors may be gathered here (see *Rumors*).

* **Rennick the Lame** (*AC 9, F2 (retired), hp 8*), a one-legged former marine, who will tell anyone who buys him drinks about the orcs' longboat and how they fight ("They row like drunks, but they board like devils").
* **Brother Silt** (*AC 7, C2, hp 9, #AT 1 mace, D 1d6, ML 8, AL C; spells: cause fear*), a soft-spoken, smiling pilgrim in gray robes, who says he has come to pray at the shrine of Saint Elmar. In truth, he is an **acolyte of the Drowned Mother**, sent by the Tidecaller to watch the newcomers and to carry messages for Corwin Pell. He wears a holy symbol of Saint Elmar openly, and a small carved fish of black stone (the Drowned Mother's sign) on a cord beneath his robe. He has 12 gp in his purse and, sewn into his robe's hem, a letter in cipher (see below). Brother Silt will be friendly and helpful, and will try to learn when the party intends to enter the Gullet. If he learns it, the cult and its allies will be warned (the DM should double guards in the caves the party plans to visit). If exposed, he will try to steal a boat in the Cauldron and flee to Lighthouse Isle, where Corwin will hide him.

*The cipher letter* is written in a simple code (each letter replaced by the next in the alphabet); any magic-user or thief can break it with an hour's work and a successful Intelligence check. It reads: "To the Keeper's Boy. Mother hungers. When the moon is dark and the sky is black, dim the King's eye. The Strake will burn the true light on Hob's Crag. — T."

**14. SMITHY AND ARMORY.** **Gunnar Velt** (*AC 7, F2, hp 12, #AT 1 hammer, D 1d6+1, ML 10, AL L*), a huge, soot-blackened, silent man, is the fortress's smith and armorer. He forges nails, chain, hooks, and anchors for the harbor, and repairs the garrison's weapons and armor. He will sell any normal weapon or armor at book price, and repair damaged armor for one-fifth its cost. Gunnar pays 15 gp for each undamaged giant crab shell brought to him; he makes a hard, light shield from them (*AC as normal shield*, weighs half as much). He has 120 gp hidden in the cold forge.

**15. THE PROVISIONER.** **Mattias Crane** (*AC 9, NM, hp 3*), a thin, fussy man with ink on his fingers, sells all normal adventuring gear at book prices, adjusted as on the price list. His stock of lanterns is small (1d4 at any time). Mattias is a terrible gossip, and anything said in his shop is all over Kingsreach by evening.

**16. THE CHANDLERY.** This shop smells of tar and rope. Its owner, **Ysolde Marrow** (*AC 9, NM, hp 5, AL N*), a sharp, gray-eyed widow, deals in ship's gear — rope, sailcloth, pitch, lamps, nets, tools — and is also the fortress's only buyer of loot. She will buy gems and jewelry at 80% of their true value, and art objects or strange items at 50%, paying in silver and gold. She keeps a small stock of oddities bought from wrecks and sailors, including:

* a brass spyglass (100 gp) that lets a character see five times as far as normal;
* a jar of whale-oil "Dragon's Breath" — a sticky oil that burns even on water (3 flasks, 10 gp each; as burning oil, but cannot be put out by water);
* a pair of goggles of smoked glass (5 gp; useless, but she claims they "let you see in the deeps").

Ysolde has 600 gp in a locked iron chest hidden beneath her floorboards and 50 gp in her till.

**17. THE FACTOR'S HOUSE.** This narrow, tall, well-kept house belongs to **Pieter Sallow** (*AC 9, NM, hp 4, AL L*), the King's Factor — the official who collects taxes, pays the garrison, keeps the accounts, and acts as moneychanger and banker. Characters may deposit money with the Factor for safekeeping (he charges 1% per month) or change one type of coin for another (for a fee of 5%). His vault, beneath the house, is guarded by 2 men-at-arms and locked with three locks; it currently holds about 4,000 gp of the King's coin and private deposits. Sallow is pompous, precise, and worried: the garrison's pay is three months behind, because the pay-chest was lost aboard the *Silver Wren* (see rumor 8). He will pay a reward of 200 gp for the recovery of that chest, which bears the royal seal, and an extra 100 gp if its contents are intact.

**18. THE MARINERS' GUILD HALL.** A fine stone hall with a carved ship over its door is the meeting place of the sailors, fishermen, and pearl divers of Kingsreach. Its master is **Captain Ottilie Brack** (*AC 7, F4, hp 22, #AT 1 cutlass, D 1d8, ML 10, AL L*), the weathered, one-eyed captain of the supply ship *Gannet* — the very woman who brought the characters to Kingsreach. When her ship is in port, Ottilie can be found here; otherwise the hall is kept by her bosun, **Gil Marlow** (*AC 7, F1, hp 7*). The guild hires out sailors (see Hirelings), and posts notices of ships expected and ships lost.

The guild has posted a **bounty on wreckers**: 50 gp for each wrecker brought in alive or dead (with proof), and 500 gp for "the captain of the wreckers, whosoever he be." The guild is not yet aware that the captain is a woman. The guild hall's strongbox holds 1,200 gp, set aside for bounties and for drowned sailors' families.

**19. CHAPEL OF SAINT ELMAR.** This small, plain chapel is dedicated to Saint Elmar, the lantern-bearer, patron of sailors and lighthouse keepers, whose sign is a lantern and a star. Its altar is a ship's bow carved from black oak, and its walls are crowded with little tin ex-votos of ships and limbs left by grateful mariners. The chapel is served by **Mother Wenna** (*AC 7, C5, hp 21, #AT 1 staff, D 1d6, ML 11, AL L; spells: cure light wounds ×2, protection from evil, bless, hold person*), a stern, white-haired priestess, and her two acolytes, **Tam** and **Corla** (both *AC 7, C1, hp 5, #AT 1 mace, D 1d6; spells: cure light wounds*).

Mother Wenna will cure wounds for any lawful or neutral character for a donation of 10 gp per spell, and will sell holy water (30 gp a vial; she has 10 vials). If a character of lawful alignment shows great courage against the cult of the Drowned Mother, she will offer him her **Lantern of Saint Elmar** (see New Magic Items) for the duration of the quest — "to be returned, child, when the dark is done."

Mother Wenna has been uneasy since Brother Silt (area 13) began coming to the chapel to pray. "He kneels like a man who has never knelt," she says. If asked directly about the Drowned Mother, she will turn pale and tell what she knows: that "the Mother in the Deep" was worshipped by wreckers and pirates on this coast long before the Realm came, that her priests drowned sacrifices at the spring tides, and that her sign is a black fish.

**20. THE BARRACKS.** A long stone building houses the garrison. On the ground floor are the mess hall, kitchens, and armory racks; above are the bunk rooms. At any time, 2d10 off-duty soldiers can be found here. The barracks are commanded by **Captain Roderic Ash** (*AC 2, F4, hp 26, #AT 1 sword, D 1d8, ML 11, AL L*), a lean, quiet officer with a scarred face, who is fiercely loyal to the Castellan and frustrated that he cannot take his men out to clear the Gullet. If the characters bring him proof of the wreckers' plot, he will lend them six marines for a single expedition (see area 27).

**21. THE SIGNAL TOWER.** At the seaward (southeastern) tip of the walls, on a walled spur joined to the bailey by a short causeway, stands a slim stone tower 50' tall with a mast on its roof. From here the garrison talks to the Kingslight on Lighthouse Isle, three leagues away across the Sound, with colored flags by day and shuttered lanterns by night. When a ship is expected, the Signal Tower flies the ship's pennant so that the lighthouse keepers will keep a sharp watch and a bright lamp. Two crossbowmen and a signalman, **Old Jory Penn** (*AC 9, NM, hp 3*), are always on duty.

Jory keeps the signal book, a ledger of every signal sent and received. A careful reader who compares it with the record of wrecks (Dagny or the Factor can supply the dates) will notice that on every night a ship was lost, the tower had flown that ship's pennant to the lighthouse the day before — and that the lighthouse had answered "light trimmed and bright." Whoever was reading the flags on Lighthouse Isle knew exactly which ship was coming and when (see Corwin Pell, area W9).

**22. WATCH TOWERS.** Four round towers stand at the corners of the curtain wall, each 40' tall. Each is manned by 2 crossbowmen by day and 3 at night, who watch the sea, the Sound, and the King's Cut. The east tower has a brass horn that is blown to signal the Sea Gate when a ship is sighted. From the top of the east tower, on a clear day, a character with a spyglass can see the mouth of the Gullet a league away — and, with luck, boats coming and going there.

**23. THE NECK WALL, THE LANDGATE, AND THE KING'S CUT.** The landward (northwest) side of Kingsreach is closed by a massive wall 40' high, above a dry ditch 60' deep and 50' wide cut clean across the promontory — the King's Cut. The King's Road leaves the fortress here through the **Landgate**, a squat gatehouse with a portcullis, and crosses the Cut on a heavy timber drawbridge raised every night at sunset by a windlass in the gatehouse. Beyond the Cut, the King's Road — an unpaved cart track, rutted by the water-carts — climbs north over the heights toward Hillnest, two leagues away (area W10). A rougher cliff path branches off northeast, loops over the heights, and comes down to the Gullet a league to the east. Six men-at-arms and a corporal hold the Landgate and patrol the Neck Wall at all times. Hillnest's water-carts and farm wagons come and go by day, and the guards know every driver by name.

The colony's founders built the Cut against the Thornwold, the trackless upland that rises beyond Hillnest, and not against any human enemy — no army has ever come over those hills. On some nights, the sentries hear things moving on the heights, and see lights; the DM may use this as a hook for later adventures.

**24. THE FISHMARKET AND SMOKEHOUSE.** A covered market where the day's catch is sold, gutted, salted, and smoked. Fresh fish costs a few coppers. The smokehouse is run by the fisherfolk, and the smell can be noticed anywhere in the fortress when the wind is from the north.

**25. THE INNER GATE.** A gatehouse with a double portcullis leads from the Outer Bailey into the Inner Ward. It is guarded by 4 men-at-arms and a corporal at all times. No one may pass without the Castellan's leave, though characters who have been summoned (or who come with news of the Gullet) will be admitted, after surrendering their weapons at the gate.

**26. THE GREAT HALL.** The inner ward's main building is a high, cold, smoky hall, its rafters hung with banners and its walls with the weapons of old campaigns. Here the Castellan dines with his officers, receives visitors, and holds court once a week to judge disputes. A tapestry behind the high table shows King Aldous the Builder pointing at the sea; the old soldiers say that when the fortress is in danger, the King's painted finger seems to move.

**27. THE CASTELLAN'S TOWER.** The tallest tower of Kingsreach, four stories high, is the home of **Sir Hollis Granmere, Castellan of Kingsreach** (*AC 2 (plate and shield), F6, hp 38, #AT 1 sword +1, D 1d8+1, MV 60' (20'), Save F6, ML 11, AL L*). Sir Hollis is a tall, gray-bearded man of sixty, stiff with old wounds and older pride. He is honest, careful, and lonely. He has held Kingsreach for twenty years and does not intend to lose it now, but he knows his garrison is too thin and he suspects that something is wrong — his letters across the sea to the King go unanswered, and the losses on the Teeth are not natural.

When the characters have been in Kingsreach a day or two, the Castellan will summon them to the Great Hall. He will thank them for coming, ask frankly about their skills, and offer them his plain terms: "Whatever you take from the Gullet is yours. I want three things in return. Bring me anything written — letters, charts, ledgers. Tell me what you see. And if you find the men who are wrecking ships on my coast, bring them to me in chains." He will also offer **100 gp for the head of the orc chief** who leads the raids on the fishing boats, and the guild bounty (area 18) applies to wreckers.

If the characters bring proof of the wreckers' plot — especially the cipher letter, Corwin Pell, or Maudie's letters from area D — Sir Hollis will reward them with 300 gp, grant them free lodging at the Salt & Anchor for as long as they stay, and lend them six marines under Captain Ash for one expedition against the Gullet. If they bring him the Tidecaller's head and the Drowned Mother's idol (see area I11), he will write to the King, and the characters will be remembered (see *Concluding the Adventure*).

The tower's upper floors hold the Castellan's private rooms, his library of sea-charts, and his treasure: a locked chest containing 1,500 gp, 300 pp, a silver-and-pearl collar worth 1,200 gp (his late wife's), and a *scroll of protection from undead*. The chest is trapped with a poison needle (save vs. Poison or die). The Castellan's personal guard is 2 marines, always outside his door.

**28. THE ARMORY.** A stone storehouse in the inner ward holds the fortress's spare arms: 60 spears, 40 swords, 20 crossbows and 2,000 bolts, 30 shields, 20 suits of chain mail, 100 arrows, and 12 barrels of pitch for the ballistae. It is always locked; the keys are held by the Castellan and Captain Ash.

**29. THE GAOL.** Beneath the Armory are four small, damp cells. At present, they hold only a drunken fisherman named **Ollie Dunt**, who stole a skin of water. If the characters bring prisoners from the Gullet, they will be kept here. Prisoners will be questioned by Captain Ash, who will share any useful information with the characters (DM's choice; a captured goblin or wrecker might reveal the layout of their lair).

**30. BALLISTA PLATFORMS.** Two great ballistae stand on platforms on the north and south sea walls, each manned by a crew of 4 men-at-arms. They can hurl iron-tipped bolts 600' out to sea (*D 2d6*, or 3d6 against boats and ships) and are used to warn off pirates. The northern ballista covers the approach to the Sea Gate. Neither has been fired in anger for twenty years, but the crews drill weekly.
