# The Caves of the Gullet

The Gullet is a narrow cove about 2,400' long, opening south onto the Sound at the bottom of the map and narrowing to a point at the top (north). Its width is about 400' at the mouth and less than 100' at the head. Cliffs ring it on the east, north, and west, rising 150' at the mouth to 200' at the head. **The East Beach**, a shingle beach 200' long that stays dry at all tides, lies just inside the mouth on the east side; a boat symbol marks it as the landing place. A dotted line marks the low-tide line along the foot of both cliffs, showing a strip of rock and sand (**the Strand**) that is exposed only at low tide and widens to a broad sandy beach at the head of the cove.

**Tide Tier caves** (marked with wavy lines to show that they flood) open at the base of the cliffs: **A** on the west wall about 400' inside the mouth; **F** on the west wall about 1,200' in; **H** at the very head of the cove, facing south; and the **Sunken Stair (I1)** in a cleft on the east side of the head, just beside H.

**The Shelf**, a natural ledge 6' to 15' wide, runs along the east cliff about 40' above high water, from the northern end of the East Beach (where a goat path climbs to it) almost to the head of the cove. Along the Shelf, from south to north, open the cave mouths **B** (goblins), **C** (orcs; with its own boat-cave **C1** at the waterline directly below), **E** (bugbears), and **J** (the Hall of the Lost Crews), the last at the Shelf's northern end.

**The Crown Tier**, near the clifftop on the east side, is reached from the Shelf by **the Ladders** — a zigzag of rope ladders and plank walks pinned to the cliff above the point between C and E. At the top of the Ladders are the mouths of **D** (the wreckers' hold) and, 150' further north, **G** (the gnoll lair). **Hob's Crag (W2)** is marked on the heights east of the cove, above the Crown Tier. The Gullet is a narrow, steep-sided cove one league (three miles) south of Kingsreach, its walls honeycombed with caves. Its mouth opens south onto the Sound. It can be reached by boat along the Sound's northern shore, or on foot by the cliff path that loops north over the heights (through 2863-115) and swings around the head of the cove to the clifftop on its eastern side; from there, a rough scramble leads down to the East Beach. Long ago it was a haunt of the pre-Realm wrecker-folk who worshipped the Drowned Mother; later, smugglers used it; for many years it was empty, except for seals. Four years ago, the Tidecaller came, and the monsters followed.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
The cliffs close in on either side as you row into the cove. The wind dies; the sound of the oars echoes back from walls of black, wet rock streaked white with gull droppings. Everywhere you look there are holes in the cliffs — dark cave mouths at the waterline, along a ledge high on the eastern wall, and higher still, near the clifftop, where ropes and ladders hang. A thin smoke drifts from one of them. On a shingle beach to your right, a gull is pecking at something that looks very much like a human hand. Somewhere above, a horn sounds once, and is silent.
</div>

**Getting In.** The characters' boat may be beached on the **East Beach**, which is above the high-water line, or rowed directly to the foot of any cave at high tide. From the East Beach, a goat path climbs 40' to the northern end of **the Shelf** (no climbing roll needed, but anyone fighting on the goat path is at –1 to hit). The Tide Tier caves can be reached on foot along the Strand only at low tide, or for about two hours either side of it by wading (waist-deep). At high tide, they can only be entered by swimming.

**Getting Noticed.** The monsters of the Gullet watch the water. Any boat entering the Gullet by day will be seen by the goblin lookouts (B1) on a 1–4 on 1d6, and by the wreckers' watch (D2) on a 1–3. A boat entering at night, in fog, or with muffled oars is seen only on a 1. Being seen does not mean being attacked — the monsters of the Gullet are not all friends, and each tribe will usually only defend its own lair — but it does mean that the tribes will be alert and that their guards will not be surprised. The goblins will send a runner to the bugbears (E) and to the Drowned Chapel (I) to report what they have seen.

**Alliances and Enemies.** The DM should keep in mind the relationships among the Gullet's inhabitants:

* The **kobolds (A)** are the lowest of the low; they pay tribute in fish and salvage to the goblins, and fear everyone.
* The **goblins (B)** serve the Drowned Mother's cult as its spies and scavengers. They pay the **bugbears (E)** to protect them. They hate the **orcs (C)**, and the orcs hate them; there have been killings on both sides.
* The **orcs (C)** are pirates who answer to no one. They trade with the **wreckers (D)** for a share of the plunder, but despise the cult and fear the undead.
* The **wreckers (D)** are the cult's partners. They pay the **gnolls (G)** to guard the high path to Hob's Crag.
* The **cult of the Drowned Mother (I)** is feared by all. The **ghouls (J)** feed on the bodies the cult leaves for them, and will not attack cultists.
* The **giant crabs (F)** and the **giant octopus (H)** are animals and attack anything.

If the characters attack one lair, the inhabitants may run to their allies for help, or the allies may come on their own — at the DM's discretion, and only if they have reason to know of the fight. The orcs will never help the goblins, and vice versa; in fact, if the goblins are badly hurt, the orcs will raid what is left of them within a day or two. A clever party may be able to provoke an outright war between the two.

**Wandering Monsters in the Gullet.** Check once every 2 turns while the party is anywhere in the Gullet, outside or inside the caves (but not in a lair whose inhabitants have been slain). Roll 1d6; an encounter occurs on a 1. Then roll 1d8:

## A. Kobold Warren

The lowest and meanest of the Gullet's lairs is a low cave in the west wall, 400' inside the mouth, whose entrance floods at high tide. Twenty-two kobolds live here, along with their females and young. They are the Gullet's drudges: they fish, gather mussels and kelp, pick over the Strand for wreckage at low tide, and hand over most of what they find to the goblins. They are cowardly, cunning, and — when cornered — vicious.

When the tide is high, the kobolds retreat to their upper caves (A3–A5), which stay dry, and the entrance is entirely flooded. They know they are safe from most enemies then, and post only one guard. At low tide, they are out and about, and much more alert.

**A1. TIDAL ENTRANCE.** (Cave mouth 10' wide and 6' high, passage 50' long sloping up to the north, opening into A2.) The kobolds have scattered **sea-urchin spines** across the floor of the passage, hidden under a few inches of water and weed. Anyone walking through without probing the floor will step on them (1 point of damage, and –1 to hit for the rest of the day from a swollen foot) unless he makes a Dexterity check. The kobolds know the safe path along the right-hand wall. At high tide, this passage is flooded to the roof.

**A2. GUARD LEDGE.** (Cave 30' × 20', passage south to A1, passage northeast to A3; a natural rock ledge 6' high along the north wall.) **6 kobolds** with slings lurk on the ledge, behind a low wall of piled stones that gives them cover (–2 to hit them with missiles). They will pelt intruders with stones, shrieking for help; in 1d3 rounds, the kobolds from A3 will come. If the attackers climb the ledge (a 6' climb, which takes one round), the guards flee to A3.

*6 Kobolds (AC 7, HD ½, hp 4, 3, 3, 2, 2, 1, #AT 1 sling or short sword, D 1d4/1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save NM, ML 6)*

Each guard carries 1d6 cp. One has a lucky gull's-egg charm painted gold (worthless).

**A3. THE DRYING CAVE.** (Cave 50' × 40', dome-roofed, passages south to A2, west to A4, and north to A5; a smoke hole in the roof.) This is the kobolds' home: a large, smoky cave hung everywhere with drying fish, seaweed, and nets, its floor littered with shells, bones, and filthy bedding. A fire of driftwood and dried kelp smolders in the center.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
The stink of fish is overpowering. Strings of drying fish hang from every crack in the rock like gray, curling banners, and smoke stings your eyes. The cave is full of small, scaly, dog-faced creatures — some armed, most not — who scatter, yipping and squealing, at the sight of you.
</div>

Here are **10 kobold warriors**, **12 kobold females** (who fight as kobolds with 2 hp each, at –1 to hit), and **15 young** (who do not fight). If the guards at A2 have called for help, the warriors will already be on their way there; otherwise, they will defend this cave while the females and young flee to A4. Hidden among the bedding is a total of 54 cp, 31 sp, and a bronze fish-hook set with a tiny garnet (worth 25 gp).

*10 Kobolds (AC 7, HD ½, hp 4, 4, 3, 3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1, #AT 1 spear, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save NM, ML 6)*

**A4. CHIEF'S DEN.** (Cave 30' × 30', passage east to A3; a curtain of fishing net hangs across the entrance.) The kobold chief, **Snaggle**, and his **4 bodyguards** dwell here, on heaps of stolen sailcloth. Snaggle is fat, sly, and greedy; he wears a lady's silk scarf as a sash and a brass ship's bell on a cord around his neck, which he rings to summon his warriors. If the fight goes against them, Snaggle will try to bargain: he will offer to tell the characters "secrets of the goblins" in return for his life. (He knows the layout of the goblin caves, B1–B6, and the secret door at B7, and that the goblins have prisoners — see B5. He will tell all of this truthfully if well treated, and then flee the Gullet with his tribe at the first chance.)

*Snaggle, kobold chief (AC 6, HD 2, hp 9, #AT 1 axe, D 1d6+1, MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 8)*
*4 Kobold bodyguards (AC 6, HD 1, hp 6, 5, 5, 4, #AT 1, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 8)*

Under Snaggle's bed of sailcloth is a small, locked iron coffer (he wears the key on his bell cord) holding 117 sp, 42 gp, a silver spoon (5 gp), and a small brass spyglass, dented but working (worth 50 gp).

**A5. LARDER AND TRIBUTE CAVE.** (Cave 30' × 20', passage south to A3; a crack in the north wall too narrow for anything larger than a rat.) This cave holds the kobolds' stores — baskets of salted fish, mussels, and kelp — and the tribute they are gathering for the goblins: a pile of salvage including a coil of good rope, a broken lantern, a cask of nails, three cutlasses (rusty), and a sealed oilskin packet. **8 giant rats**, which the kobolds feed and keep as pets (and, occasionally, eat), lair among the baskets. They attack anyone who is not a kobold.

*8 Giant rats (AC 7, HD ½, hp 3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1, #AT 1 bite, D 1d3 + disease (1 in 20 chance), MV 120' (40'), swim 60' (20'), Save NM, ML 8)*

The oilskin packet came from the wreck of the *Merrow*. It holds a merchant's bill of lading listing the *Merrow*'s cargo (bales of silk and fine wine, 12 chests of silver plate, and "1 strongbox, sealed, for the Factor at Kingsreach") and a letter from the merchant's wife. The silver plate and the strongbox are now in the wreckers' hold (D4 and D7). Proof of the cargo will be of great interest to the Factor and to the Castellan.

## B. Goblin Lair

Halfway along the Shelf, a broad cave mouth opens in the east cliff, with a rough curtain wall of piled stones across its front. This is the lair of the **Red Gull** goblins, who are named for the gulls they kill and dye red to hang outside their door. The goblins are the cult's eyes and ears: they watch the Gullet from their lookout posts, scavenge the Strand and the wrecks, and sort the salvage, sending the best of it to the Drowned Chapel. They are cruel, numerous, and well-organized, and they have more to lose than the kobolds.

**B1. THE GULL WALL.** (Cave mouth 30' wide, with a drystone wall 5' high across it and a 5'-wide gap at its north end; inside, a vestibule 30' × 20' leads east to B2.) Six dead gulls, dyed red, hang from cords along the top of the wall. Behind the wall, **2 goblin lookouts** watch the Gullet at all times. They will see anyone approaching along the Shelf (unless the approach is magically hidden), and will shout a warning that brings the guards from B2 in one round.

Above the wall, on a ledge 20' up, the goblins have stacked a pile of loose boulders behind a wooden prop. If the lookouts pull a rope, the boulders come crashing down onto the Shelf in front of the cave mouth, doing 2d6 damage to anyone in a 20' × 10' area (save vs. Paralysis for half). The trap can be used only once, and the goblins will use it as soon as three or more enemies are in the area.

*2 Goblin lookouts (AC 6, HD 1–1, hp 5, 3, #AT 1 short bow or spear, D 1d6, MV 60' (20'), Save NM, ML 7)*

**B2. GUARD ROOM.** (Cave 30' × 30', passages west to B1, east to B3, and north to B4.) **6 goblin guards** squat around a brazier here, gambling with knucklebones for copper pieces (32 cp on the floor). If alerted, they rush to the Gull Wall. If they are losing a fight, two will run for B6 to alert the king, while the others fall back toward B4.

*6 Goblin guards (AC 6, HD 1–1, hp 6, 5, 4, 4, 3, 2, #AT 1 short sword or spear, D 1d6, MV 60' (20'), Save NM, ML 7)*

**B3. SALVAGE HALL.** (Cave 50' × 30', passage west to B2, passage north to B5, passage east to B6.) This long cave is piled high with everything the goblins have scavenged from wrecks and drowned men: heaps of sodden cloth, broken barrels, coils of rope, spars, chain, oars, boots, belts, buckles, buttons, and bones. **8 goblins** are always busy here, sorting the salvage under the eye of a **goblin overseer** with a whip; the two prisoners from B5 are also here by day (see B5), chained by the ankle and sorting with the goblins.

*8 Goblins (AC 6, HD 1–1, hp 5, 5, 4, 4, 3, 3, 2, 2, #AT 1, D 1d6, MV 60' (20'), Save NM, ML 7)*
*Goblin overseer (AC 6, HD 1, hp 7, #AT 1 whip or club, D 1d4/1d6, ML 8)*

The salvage is mostly worthless, but a careful search (2 turns for one character; each additional character reduces the time by 1 turn, to a minimum of 1) will reveal: a ship's brass sextant (worth 60 gp), a pair of silver candlesticks (25 gp each), a woman's cloak of fine blue wool with a silver brooch (cloak 15 gp, brooch 30 gp), a bag of 200 mixed coins (110 cp, 60 sp, 30 gp), and a waterlogged wooden case holding three potion flasks, two of which are broken; the third is a *potion of healing*. The overseer carries the key to the prisoners' chains.

**B4. COMMON CAVE.** (Cave 60' × 40', irregular, with a low roof; passage south to B2; a narrow, half-dug passage leads northeast for 40' and ends in rubble — the goblins' side of a tunnel that the orcs are digging toward them from C4. Neither side knows exactly how close the other is.) This is where most of the goblins live. Here are **10 goblin warriors**, **12 females** (fight as goblins with 3 hp each, at –1 to hit), and **9 young** (who do not fight). The cave is filthy, smoky, and crowded with sleeping mats of rotten sailcloth.

*10 Goblins (AC 6, HD 1–1, hp 6, 5, 5, 4, 4, 4, 3, 3, 2, 1, #AT 1, D 1d6, MV 60' (20'), Save NM, ML 7)*

Scattered through the bedding is a total of 140 cp, 88 sp, and 12 gp. If the characters listen at the rubble at the end of the northeast passage, they will hear (on a 1–2 on 1d6, or 1–3 for dwarves) the faint tap of picks — the orcs, digging.

**B5. THE PENS.** (Cave 20' × 20', closed by a gate of lashed spars and rope, passage south to B3.) The goblins keep their prisoners here at night, and in the Salvage Hall by day. There are two:

* **Pell Tallow** (*AC 9, NM, hp 2 (normally 4)*), Wren Tallow's older brother, who was taken alive in the goblin attack on the pearl divers last spring. He is gaunt, filthy, and half-starved, but still has his wits. Pell has overheard much: he knows that the goblins answer to "the priest in the deep," that they are paid by "the dead man's master" to watch for boats, and that "something big is coming at the next storm tide — the goblins are all to go down to the Chapel and fight for the Mother." If rescued, he will be eternally grateful, and Wren Tallow will become the characters' devoted friend (and will work for them for half pay).
* **Davey Rook** (*AC 9, NM, hp 3*), a ship's boy from the *Merrow*, the only survivor of her crew (the rest are now brine dead or worse). Davey saw the "second light" on the night of the wreck, and also saw the wreckers come down to the beach afterward, led by "a woman with a burned face and a big hat." He knows nothing else. If returned to Kingsreach, he is valuable proof for the Castellan.

**B6. HALL OF THE GOBLIN KING.** (Cave 40' × 30', passage west to B3; a curtain of ship's flags across the entrance; secret door in the north wall leading to B7.) The goblin king, **Grubnash**, sits on a throne made from a ship's figurehead (a carved, painted woman with a broken nose) set on a platform of sea chests. With him are his **4 bodyguards** and his **2 wives** (fight as goblins with 3 hp each).

<div class="box" markdown="1">
Ragged flags of a dozen ships hang from the roof of this cave. At its far end, on a platform of sea chests, a carved and painted wooden woman — a ship's figurehead — has been made into a throne, and in her lap sits a large, warty goblin in a tarnished brass breastplate far too big for him, a ship's officer's hat on his head and a cutlass across his knees. "Well, well," he says, in passable common. "More fish for the Mother."
</div>

Grubnash is cunning and boastful. If the fight goes against him, he will offer the characters "a great secret" for his life: the location of the secret door to the bugbears (B7), and the fact that the cult is gathering an army for "the storm-tide." He is quite willing to betray the bugbears and the cult. However, he will also try to lead the characters into the bugbear cave (E) as a trap.

*Grubnash, goblin king (AC 4, HD 3, hp 15, #AT 1 cutlass, D 1d8, MV 60' (20'), Save F3, ML 9 — the morale of all goblins in the lair is 9 while he lives)*
*4 Goblin bodyguards (AC 5, HD 2, hp 11, 10, 9, 8, #AT 1, D 1d6+1, MV 60' (20'), Save F2, ML 9)*

The sea chests of the platform hold the goblin treasure. Two are empty; the third holds 600 sp and 210 gp; the fourth is locked and trapped with a spring-loaded blade (1d6 damage to the opener unless the trap is found and removed) and holds 90 pp, a gold-and-amber necklace (worth 400 gp), a silver flask of brandy (worth 30 gp), and a *scroll of protection from undead*. The figurehead itself is worth 100 gp to Arn Keel, who will recognize it as that of the *Silver Wren*. Under the throne, wrapped in oilcloth, is a note scratched on a thin slate in the common tongue: "*King of Gulls. Watch the water. Tell me of every boat. When the storm-tide comes, all your folk to the Chapel. The Mother will feed you. — T.*"

**B7. TRIBUTE PASSAGE.** (Secret door in the north wall of B6, opening into a twisting passage 5' wide and 120' long, leading north to the guard cave of the bugbears, E2. The passage ends in a door of planks barred from the bugbear side.) Halfway along the passage, the goblins leave their payments for the bugbears — food, drink, and a share of the salvage — in a niche in the wall. At present, the niche holds a cask of ale, a side of salted pork, and a sack of 300 cp. Knocking on the plank door at the end of the passage will bring a bugbear to the door, who will open it for anyone who answers in the goblin tongue. Characters who do not speak goblin will be attacked by all the bugbears in E2.

## C. Orc Lair

A third of the way along the Shelf, beyond the goblins' wall, the cliff is pierced by a tall, narrow cave mouth. Directly below it, at the waterline, a much larger sea cave opens; this is the orcs' boat-cave, where their longboat is moored. The two are joined by a rope stair inside the cliff. The orcs of the **Broken Oar** are pirates: they row out in their longboat to raid fishing boats and lone merchantmen, and they trade some of their plunder to the wreckers for weapons and drink. They are bold, greedy, and quarrelsome, and they hate the goblins with a passion.

**C1. THE BOAT CAVE.** (Sea cave 60' × 40', open to the Gullet on the west side; the far end has a rough rock jetty 20' long; a rope stair leads up through a crack in the east wall to C2.) At high and middle tide, the orcs' longboat, the ***Gutting Knife*** — black-painted and patched, with sixteen oars — floats here at the jetty. At low tide, it rests on its keel in the mud and cannot be moved. Two **orc boat-guards** lounge on the jetty with a jug of grog.

*2 Orc guards (AC 6, HD 1, hp 6, 4, #AT 1, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 8)*

The *Gutting Knife* is a longboat in poor condition (worth 200 gp). In its stern locker are a spare cutlass, a horn, a bag of 40 sp, and a crude chart showing the location of the lizard men's mound in the Fennmouth (marked with a skull) and of Seal Skerry (marked "FISHMEN — KEEP OFF").

**C2. ROPE STAIR.** (Natural chimney 10' wide, 40' high, with a rope ladder and knotted ropes, connecting C1 below with C3 above.) Climbing the stair takes 2 rounds. A character fighting from the ladder is at –2 to hit and has no Dexterity bonus to AC. A bell hangs from a cord at the top; anyone climbing without first giving the password ("Black Water") has a 3 in 6 chance of jostling it, which brings the guards from C3.

**C3. GUARD POST.** (Cave 30' × 20', entrance from the Shelf to the west, chimney to C2 in the floor of the southwest corner, passage east to C4.) **4 orc guards** are always on watch here, two at the cave mouth and two by the chimney. They are suspicious and quick to fight. One will run to C4 for help as soon as there is trouble.

*4 Orc guards (AC 6, HD 1, hp 7, 6, 5, 4, #AT 1 cutlass or spear, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 8)*

**C4. MESS HALL.** (Cave 50' × 40', passages west to C3, north to C5, east to C6 and C7; a rough passage leads south for 30' to a working face where the orcs are digging toward the goblins at B4.) A long table made from a ship's hatch-cover dominates this cave, surrounded by casks used as stools. **10 orcs** are here at any time, eating, drinking, brawling, or digging at the south passage. Their weapons are close to hand.

*10 Orcs (AC 6, HD 1, hp 8, 7, 6, 6, 5, 5, 4, 4, 3, 2, #AT 1, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 8)*

Each orc carries 2d6 sp. A barrel of strong grog stands at the end of the table. If the characters can provoke the orcs into thinking that the goblins are attacking — for example, by planting goblin weapons or red-dyed gull feathers on orc bodies — the orcs will break through into B4 within a day and fight a bloody war that leaves both tribes at half strength.

**C5. FAMILY CAVE.** (Cave 40' × 30', passage south to C4.) Here are **8 orc females** and **6 young**. The females fight as orcs (hp 4 each, at –1 to hit) if attacked or if their young are threatened; the young do not fight. There is nothing of value here but some copper jewelry (worth 20 gp in all).

**C6. STOREROOM.** (Cave 30' × 30', passage west to C4; the entrance is closed by a stout door of ship's planking, locked; the bosuns at C7 hold the key.) The orcs keep their plunder here: a bale of fine silk (worth 300 gp, weight 400 coins), two casks of brandy (worth 50 gp each), a chest of smoked fish, 12 cutlasses, 6 shields, a coil of chain, and, nested carefully in a basket of wet seaweed, **two large leathery eggs** — the lizard men's stolen eggs (see area W6). The orcs intend to eat them at the next feast. The eggs are worth nothing to anyone but the lizard men, who will be grateful beyond measure if they are returned unharmed.

**C7. BOSUNS' QUARTERS.** (Cave 30' × 20', passage west to C4, door north to C8.) The two orc bosuns, **Ruk** and **Snagtooth**, live here with their favorite weapons and their loot. They will come at once if there is fighting in C4.

*2 Orc bosuns (AC 5, HD 2, hp 13, 11, #AT 1 axe, D 1d8, MV 120' (40'), Save F2, ML 9)*

Ruk has 37 gp and the key to C6. Snagtooth has 22 gp and a gold earring worth 40 gp. Under their bunks is a small chest holding 220 sp.

**C8. THE CAPTAIN'S CABIN.** (Cave 30' × 30', door south to C7.) The orc chief, **Captain Gorrack Half-Hat**, has made this cave into a crude imitation of a ship's cabin, with a hammock, a sea chest, a table, and a stolen ship's lantern. Gorrack is big, scarred, and clever for an orc. He wears a sea captain's plumed hat, half of which has been burned away (hence his name), and a coat of rusty chain. He is the orc chief whose head the Castellan wants (area 27).

## D. The Wreckers' Hold

High on the east cliff, near the head of the Gullet, a rope-and-plank stair called the Ladders climbs from the Shelf to a ledge just below the clifftop. Here, behind a wall of mortared stone with a single heavy door, the wreckers have made their stronghold in an old smugglers' cave. There are nineteen wreckers in all, led by **Black Maudie Strake**. They are hard, desperate men and women — escaped convicts, deserters, ruined sailors — who would cut a throat for a silver piece. Maudie rules them by fear and by sharing out plunder fairly.

**THE LADDERS.** From the Shelf, a zigzag of rope ladders and plank walkways pinned to the cliff with iron spikes climbs 100' to the Crown. Climbing takes 3 turns. The planks are rotten in places; each character climbing must make a Dexterity check (once) or a plank breaks under him, and he must save vs. Paralysis to catch a rope, or fall 1d4 × 10' (1d6 damage per 10'). Characters on the Ladders are in plain view of the watch gallery (D2), and may be shot at from above.

**D1. THE DOOR IN THE CLIFF.** (Ledge 40' long and 10' wide at the top of the Ladders; a stone wall 15' high closes a cave mouth, with an iron-bound oak door in its center and two arrow slits; inside is a short entry passage 10' × 20' leading east to D3. A narrow path leads north along the ledge for 150' to G1.) The door is barred from within. It can be broken down only with great effort (a total of 3 successful open-doors rolls). Knocking will bring a guard to the slit, who will demand the password ("Two Lights") and a name. The wreckers are expecting messages from Corwin Pell and from the cult; a clever bluff may get the door opened.

**D2. WATCH GALLERY.** (Gallery 50' long and 10' wide running along the cliff face south of D1, with four arrow slits looking out over the Gullet; door at its north end into the entry passage.) **3 wreckers** with light crossbows watch the Gullet and the Ladders from here at all times. They will shoot at anyone climbing the Ladders without being expected, and will shout to D3 for help.

*3 Wrecker crossbowmen (AC 7, F1, hp 6, 5, 4, #AT 1 light crossbow or short sword, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 7)*

**D3. COMMON HALL.** (Cave 50' × 40', passage west to D1, passages east to D4 and D5, north to D6, and northeast to D7.) This large, smoky cave is the wreckers' barracks and dining hall, with bunks of ship's planking along the walls, a long table, and a fire pit. **8 wreckers** are here, sleeping, eating, or gambling, with weapons at hand.

*8 Wreckers (AC 7, F1, hp 7, 6, 6, 5, 5, 4, 3, 3, #AT 1 cutlass, club, or knife, D 1d6, MV 120' (40'), Save F1, ML 7)*

Each wrecker carries 1d10 sp and 1d4 gp. Under one loose bunk-plank is a hidden purse with 55 gp and a small pearl (worth 50 gp).

**D4. PLUNDER CAVE.** (Cave 40' × 30', passage west to D3; a locked door — Maudie has the key.) This is where the wreckers store the plunder they have not yet sold to the orcs or smuggled away: four bales of wet-damaged cloth (worthless), three casks of fine wine (40 gp each), a crate of tea (worth 200 gp, weight 300 coins), a chest of **silver plate from the *Merrow*** (twenty pieces worth 30 gp each, 600 gp in all, weight 600 coins), and a long box holding six fine crossbows (worth 50 gp each). A crude ledger nailed to the wall lists the shares owed to each wrecker, to "the Fish-Priest," to "Gorrack," and to "the Lamp" (Corwin Pell).

**D5. THE WITCH'S NOOK.** (Cave 20' × 20', passage west to D3; curtain of seal-skins across the entrance.) **Old Nan Gorse**, Maudie's hedge-witch and navigator, lives here among bundles of dried weeds, jars of pickled things, and heaps of charts. Nan is a gaunt old woman with one milky eye and a seagull perched on her shoulder. She reads the weather and the tides for the wreckers, and she will use her magic to defend the hold.

*Old Nan Gorse (AC 9, MU2, hp 6, #AT 1 dagger, D 1d4, MV 120' (40'), Save MU2, ML 7; spells: sleep, ventriloquism)*

Nan casts *sleep* at the first group of intruders she sees, then uses *ventriloquism* to confuse them ("They're behind you!") while she flees to Maudie. Her spell book, hidden in a sealed jar of pickled eels, holds *sleep*, *ventriloquism*, *read magic*, *light*, and *detect magic*. She has a *potion of gaseous form* and 34 gp. Among her charts is the best map of the Teeth ever made, showing the safe channels (worth 100 gp to any captain, and making the Teeth safe to navigate for anyone who uses it).

**D6. JOSS'S ROOM.** (Cave 20' × 20', passage south to D3.) **Scupper Joss**, Maudie's second-in-command, a big, bald, cheerful brute with a boarding axe, lives here with his pet monkey (a capuchin from some western wreck; harmless). Joss will come to any fight at once, roaring.

*Scupper Joss (AC 5, F3, hp 19, #AT 1 boarding axe, D 1d8, MV 90' (30'), Save F3, ML 9)*

In a chest under his bunk: 140 gp, a silver-chased pistol-crossbow of foreign make (a hand crossbow, range 30/60/90, D 1d4; worth 100 gp), and a bottle of rum.

**D7. MAUDIE'S CABIN.** (Cave 30' × 30', passage southwest to D3; door of oak, locked; a narrow passage leads north from the back of the cave to D8.) This cave is surprisingly comfortable: a carpet, a great carved bed from some captain's cabin, a writing desk, a brass lamp, and racks of charts and weapons. **Black Maudie Strake** is here unless she has been alerted. Maudie is a tall woman of forty, her face seared on one side by an old burn, dressed in a long black coat and a broad-brimmed hat. She fights with a cutlass and a dagger, and she is the most dangerous of the wreckers.

## E. Bugbear Grotto

Between the orc lair and the Hall of the Lost Crews, a cave mouth on the Shelf is curtained with a great old sail, gray and patched, painted with the words **WELCOME TRAVELERS — FOOD AND REST** in crude common letters. This is the home of a band of bugbears, hulking, hairy goblin-kin who serve the goblins as hired muscle. They are clever enough to know that a friendly-looking door draws in more prey than a guarded one.

**E1. THE WELCOMING CAVE.** (Cave 30' × 30', entrance from the Shelf to the west curtained with a sail; passage east to E2.) A fire burns in a ring of stones, with a pot of fish stew bubbling over it. A rough table holds wooden bowls, and there are benches with blankets. A large, hairy humanoid in a filthy leather apron stands stirring the pot. He grins, showing yellow fangs, and in very bad common says: "Welcome, friends! Eat, sit! Very nice stew!"

This bugbear is the band's "cook," and his job is to keep visitors talking while his comrades in E2 creep up behind them. The bugbears surprise on a 1–3 on 1d6 even when the characters are watchful; if the characters accept the hospitality and sit down to eat, the attackers from E2 automatically surprise them. The stew is real and edible (and not poisoned — the bugbears want their meat unspoiled).

*Bugbear cook (AC 5, HD 3+1, hp 15, #AT 1 cleaver, D 2d4, MV 90' (30'), Save F3, ML 9)*

**E2. GUARD CAVE.** (Cave 40' × 30', passage west to E1, passage east to E3, passage north to E4; a plank door in the south wall, barred from this side, opens on the tribute passage to the goblin king, B7.) **3 bugbears** wait here, listening to the conversation in E1. When the cook bangs his ladle on the pot, they creep out to attack. If the cook is attacked, they come at once.

*3 Bugbears (AC 5, HD 3+1, hp 18, 16, 13, #AT 1 morning star or club, D 2d4, MV 90' (30'), Save F3, ML 9)*

Each has 2d10 gp. In a corner, under a pile of furs, is a sack of 400 sp — the goblins' last payment.

**E3. CHIEFTAIN'S CAVE.** (Cave 40' × 40', passage west to E2.) The chief of the bugbears, **Gorm**, and his mate live here, on a bed of furs and sails. Gorm is huge, cunning, and lazy. He has a deal with the goblins, but no great love for them, and he can be bargained with: if the characters offer him more than the goblins pay (at least 200 gp, he will say, and "meat"), he will agree to stay out of their fight with the goblins — and might even lend a bugbear or two to fight the goblins, for a price. He will, of course, betray the characters the moment it suits him.

*Gorm, bugbear chief (AC 4, HD 5+1, hp 30, #AT 1 two-handed axe, D 2d4+2, MV 90' (30'), Save F5, ML 10)*
*Bugbear female (AC 5, HD 3, hp 14, #AT 1 bite/club, D 1d8, ML 9)*

Gorm keeps his treasure in a great iron-bound chest (locked; he has the key on a thong around his wrist): 1,200 cp, 800 sp, 300 gp, a silver mirror worth 70 gp, a jeweled scabbard (empty) worth 250 gp, and a *potion of giant strength*. A crude wooden cage in the corner holds a gray seal pup, which Gorm is fattening up; it is harmless.

**E4. YOUNG AND LARDER.** (Cave 30' × 20', passage south to E2, passage east to E5.) **4 bugbear young** (*AC 6, HD 1, hp 5 each, #AT 1 bite, D 1d4, ML 7*) play here among heaps of bones and gnawed driftwood. They will shriek for help if they see intruders, and bite if cornered.

**E5. THE TUB.** (Cave 20' × 20', passage west to E4; the floor is a natural rock basin, half full of seawater.) In this basin, chained by the wrist to an iron ring in the rock, is a **merman** named **Kelso** (*AC 7, HD 1, hp 2 (normally 6)*), captured by the bugbears in a net two weeks ago. They are keeping him fresh for a feast. Kelso is weak from hunger and from the water, which is foul and stagnant, but he can speak a little of the common tongue. If freed and carried back to the sea, he will swim to Seal Skerry and tell his people of the characters' kindness: the mermen of W5 will treat the party as friends, granting a +3 bonus on all reaction rolls, and Thallo will give them **two pearls of water breathing** freely as a reward. Kelso also knows of the Mother's Throat (area I12) and can tell the characters how to find it from the sea.
