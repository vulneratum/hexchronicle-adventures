## F. Crab Grotto

Two-thirds of the way into the Gullet, a wide, low cave opens at the base of the west cliff, its floor a maze of tide pools. It floods to the roof at high tide; at low tide, it can be entered on foot from the Strand. The cave is home to a family of giant crabs and something larger. No intelligent creature lives here, and the goblins and kobolds avoid it.

**F1. TIDE POOL CAVE.** (Cave 60' × 40', open to the Strand on the east side; passage 10' wide leads west to F2; a sloping chimney in the north wall leads up to F4.) The floor is a jumble of rocks and pools, slippery with weed: characters moving faster than half speed must make a Dexterity check each round or fall. **2 giant crabs** lurk in the deepest pools, their shells covered in weed and barnacles; they surprise on a 1–4 on 1d6.

*2 Giant crabs (AC 2, HD 3, hp 17, 13, #AT 2 pincers, D 2d6/2d6, MV 60' (20'), Save F2, ML 7)*

Their shells, if undamaged (the crabs must be slain by blows to the legs and eyes — ask the players how they fight!), can be sold to the smith at Kingsreach for 15 gp each.

**F2. THE BONE POOL.** (Cave 40' × 40', passage east to F1; the floor is a single deep pool, 6' deep at low tide, with a rim of rock 5' wide around three sides.) The bottom of the pool is littered with bones and old wreckage. Lurking among it is a **shellback**, an enormous hermit crab that has made its home in the skull of a whale (see New Monsters). It will rise from the pool to attack anyone who walks around its rim.

*Shellback (AC 1 (shell) / 6 (body), HD 4, hp 22, #AT 2 pincers, D 1d8/1d8, MV 60' (20'), swim 30' (10'), Save F2, ML 8)*

In the bottom of the pool, among the bones, are the remains of a pearl diver. His bag still holds 7 pearls (worth 50 gp each) and a bronze knife. One of the bones is a skull with a silver tooth (1 gp). Searching the pool takes 2 turns and requires someone to dive.

**F3. CRAB NURSERY.** (Side cave 20' × 30' off the south side of F2, entered only by a submerged opening 4' across, 6' underwater even at low tide; the cave beyond is above water and dry.) Hundreds of tiny crabs, no bigger than a thumbnail, crawl over every surface of this cave. They are harmless. In a crack in the back wall (found on a 1–2 on 1d6) is a smuggler's bundle left here forty years ago: an oilskin packet containing 50 pp and a *ring of water walking*.

**F4. THE DRY LOFT.** (Cave 20' × 30' reached by a sloping chimney 30' long from the north side of F1; this cave is above even the spring high tide.) Old smugglers once used this cave as a hiding place, and a rotting wooden chest still stands against the wall. It is covered by a thick patch of **yellow mold**. Anyone touching the chest or the mold releases a cloud of spores (everyone within 10' must save vs. Death Ray or take 1d6 damage per round for 6 rounds, or until washed in seawater).

*Yellow mold (AC 9, HD 2, hp 10, #AT spores, D 1d6 + choking, MV 0, Save F2, ML n/a; burned by fire)*

The chest, if the mold is burned away, holds 300 gp in old coin, a rusty cutlass, and a waterlogged book — a smuggler's log describing "the Old Priests' cave at the head of the Gullet" and "a stair that only shows at low water."

## G. Gnoll Lair

North of the wreckers' hold, along a narrow ledge path at the top of the east cliff, a ragged cave mouth opens just below the clifftop. A pack of gnolls — hyena-headed humanoids, savage and hungry — lives here, paid by the wreckers in meat and plunder to guard the high path to Hob's Crag. They are bullies who prey on the goblins and kobolds when they can, and they treat the wreckers with a sullen, dangerous respect.

**G1. THE HIGH PATH.** (Cave mouth 20' wide at the north end of the ledge path from D1; inside, a cave 30' × 20' with a rough stair of piled stones leading up through a cleft to the clifftop near Hob's Crag; passage east to G2.) Gnawed bones and broken skulls are scattered around the cave mouth as a warning. **2 gnoll guards** sit at the foot of the stone stair, watching both the ledge and the clifftop. If they see intruders, one will bark an alarm while the other fights.

*2 Gnolls (AC 5, HD 2, hp 11, 9, #AT 1 spear, D 2d4, MV 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 8)*

**G2. GUARD DEN.** (Cave 30' × 30', passage west to G1, passages north to G3 and east to G4.) **4 gnolls** lounge here among piles of bones. They will be at the fight in G1 within 2 rounds of an alarm.

*4 Gnolls (AC 5, HD 2, hp 13, 10, 9, 7, #AT 1 sword or spear, D 2d4, MV 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 8)*

Each carries 2d6 gp. A broken chest holds 230 sp and a dented silver cup (15 gp).

**G3. THE DEN.** (Cave 40' × 30', passage south to G2.) The pack's females and young live here: **3 gnoll females** (fight as gnolls, hp 8 each, D 1d6) and **5 young** (non-combatants). The cave stinks of rot; the floor is ankle-deep in old bones and filthy hides. Buried under the hides are a pair of fine boots (they are *elven boots*), taken from the adventurer party whose bones lie in the pines at Spider Cove (W7) — one of them fled here, and the gnolls caught him.

**G4. THE LEADER'S CAVE.** (Cave 30' × 30', passage west to G2; a crack in the east wall leads to G5.) The gnoll leader, **Rakkha**, a great, scarred beast with a mane of filthy gray hair, lairs here with his **2 bodyguards**.

*Rakkha, gnoll leader (AC 4, HD 3, hp 20, #AT 1 two-handed sword, D 2d4+2, MV 90' (30'), Save F3, ML 9)*
*2 Gnoll bodyguards (AC 5, HD 2, hp 14, 12, #AT 1, D 2d4, MV 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 9)*

Rakkha keeps his treasure in a stinking leather sack under his bed of furs: 400 ep, 160 gp, a gold torc (worth 300 gp), and a *wand of magic detection* with 8 charges, which he thinks is a magic stick for scratching his back.

**G5. THE WAILING CRACK.** (A narrow crack 3' wide and 60' long, twisting east from G4 and ending in a small cave 10' × 20', open to the sky through a hole in the roof.) The wind howls through this crack day and night with a sound like a woman screaming. In the small cave at the end is the nest of **4 stirges**, which feed on the gnolls' leftovers. Among their droppings are 13 gp, a silver ring (10 gp), and a *potion of levitation*.

*4 Stirges (AC 7, HD 1*, hp 6, 5, 3, 2, #AT 1 beak, D 1d3 + blood drain, MV 30' (10'), fly 180' (60'), Save F2, ML 9)*

## H. The Kraken's Kettle

At the very head of the Gullet, where the two cliffs come together, a great dark arch opens at the waterline. At low tide, the sea drains partly out of it, leaving a broad, deep pool under a domed roof; at high tide, the arch is entirely underwater. The fisherfolk call it the Kraken's Kettle, and with good reason: a **giant octopus** lives here.

**H1. THE KETTLE.** (Domed cave 80' across; at low tide, a pool 60' across and 20' deep fills its center, surrounded by a rim of slippery rock 10' wide; at high tide, the whole cave is underwater to within 5' of the roof. An underwater tunnel leads from the bottom of the pool to H2; the lizard men's tunnel from the Fennmouth enters on the east side, underwater.)

<div class="box" markdown="1">
Water drips and echoes in the green gloom. A great round pool fills the cave, still and black, its surface broken only by the drip from the roof. Around its edge, a rim of rock glistens with weed. On the far side, half in the water, something glints — a heap of metal, coins perhaps, or armor. Then the black water bulges, and a long, pale, sucker-lined arm slides silently up onto the rock.
</div>

**Many-Arms**, as the merfolk call it, is a giant octopus 20' across. It will attack anyone on the rim of the pool, seizing victims with its tentacles and dragging them into the water. A character hit by a tentacle is held, and will be dragged into the pool the next round unless he makes a successful Strength check or someone severs the tentacle (each tentacle is severed by 6 points of damage, which does not count against the octopus's hit points). Anyone in the water is at –4 to hit and may drown (see *Swimming*). If reduced to fewer than half its hit points, the octopus will squirt a cloud of black ink and retreat to H2.

*Many-Arms, giant octopus (AC 7, HD 4, hp 24, #AT 8 tentacles / 1 bite, D 1d3 each / 1d6, MV swim 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 7)*

The glinting heap is the octopus's treasure, gathered from drowned men: 1,100 sp, 280 gp, a gilded breastplate (*plate mail +1*, sized for a man), a jeweled dagger (worth 150 gp), and a small sealed lead box holding a *scroll of cure light wounds × 3* (for clerics).

**H2. THE INNER POOL.** (Small cave 30' across, entirely flooded, reached only by a 40' underwater tunnel from the bottom of H1.) This is Many-Arms's den, a pitch-black flooded grotto. There is nothing here but bones — except in one corner, where a narrow underwater crack (only 2' wide, too small for the octopus) leads to H3.

**H3. THE DROWNED CRACK.** (A flooded crack 2' wide and 80' long, rising steadily, leading from H2 to the Pool of Rising, area I5.) A slim character with water-breathing magic, or one who can hold his breath for 8 rounds or more, may squeeze through this crack and surface in the pool at I5, in the heart of the Drowned Chapel — left in the middle of the brine dead.

## I. The Drowned Chapel

Beneath the head of the Gullet, in caves carved out by the sea and enlarged by the hands of a forgotten people, lies the sanctuary of the Drowned Mother. It was old when the Realm was young. For centuries it lay empty and half-flooded, until Sedge Harrow — a renegade priest driven out of a temple far to the south for "studies unfit for the faithful" — found it four years ago, and woke what slept there.

The Chapel can be entered in three ways:

* by the **Sunken Stair** (I1), whose entrance is exposed only at low tide;
* by the **Mother's Throat** (I12), an underwater tunnel from the open sea, which can be swum only at a spring low tide or with water-breathing magic;
* by the **Drowned Crack** from the Kraken's Kettle (H3), or by the **corpse-chute** from the Hall of the Lost Crews (J4).

**Inside the Chapel, all areas are above water** except where noted. The air is cold and damp, and smells of salt and rot. The walls are carved everywhere with waves, fish, drowning men, and the vast, many-armed, female shape of the Drowned Mother. There is no light except where noted.

The Chapel's inhabitants are the **Tidecaller**, his **2 adepts** and **4 acolytes**, and the dead they command. If the alarm is raised, the acolytes will gather at I3, the adepts will join the Tidecaller at I8, and the Tidecaller will retreat to the Sanctuary (I11) to make his stand.

**I1. THE SUNKEN STAIR.** (A cleft in the east cliff at the head of the Gullet, just east of the Kettle, filled with water at all tides but low. At low tide, a stair of 40 broad, weed-slick steps is revealed, carved in the rock and descending into the cliff, then rising again to a landing 20' × 20' where a great arch opens north into I2. At the bottom of the dip, the stair passes beneath an arch carved as a gaping fish's mouth.)

<div class="box" markdown="1">
As the sea draws back, a black cleft in the cliff empties itself in streaming waterfalls, revealing broad steps carved in the living rock, slimy with weed, leading down into darkness. Over the stair arches a great carved fish's head, its mouth gaping, its stone eyes inlaid with something that gleams dully in the gray light. The steps go down beneath the fish's jaws — and then, beyond, you can just see them rising again.
</div>

The stair is passable for **2 hours** at low tide (and for 1 hour either side, by wading or swimming with the current). After that, the dip in the stair floods, and anyone inside the Chapel is trapped until the next low tide — unless they swim the flooded section (60' underwater) or find another way out. **The DM should make sure the players know the tide is coming in**, by describing the water rising on the steps behind them when they enter.

The eyes of the carved fish are two disks of mother-of-pearl (worth 25 gp each). Prying them out is easy, but anyone who does so must save vs. Spells or be marked by the Drowned Mother: until the mark is removed by a *remove curse*, the brine dead will always attack that character first, and he is at –1 on all saving throws against cold and drowning.

**I2. THE GATE OF SHELLS.** (Hall 30' × 40', arch south to I1, great double doors north to I3.) The walls of this hall are covered entirely in shells — mussels, scallops, whelks, oysters — pressed into the plaster in patterns of waves and eyes. The double doors to the north are of black, sea-hardened oak, carved with the shape of a woman with eight arms. They are not locked. On either side of them stand **2 brine dead**, motionless as statues, the drowned crew of the *Merrow*. They attack anyone who is not wearing the black stone fish of the cult.

*2 Brine dead (AC 7, HD 2, hp 13, 10, #AT 1 grab, D 1d6 + drowning grip, MV 90' (30'), swim 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 12; see New Monsters)*

**I3. HALL OF OFFERINGS.** (Hall 60' × 40', with a vaulted roof 20' high supported by two rows of three pillars carved as twisting kelp; double doors south to I2; doors east to I4 and west to I6; a broad passage north to I5. A raised dais 20' × 10' stands in the center of the north wall.)

Here the cult receives its offerings from the goblins and the wreckers. The pillars are hung with the belongings of the drowned — coats, hats, boots, belts, lockets, rings — as if on a monstrous coat-rack. On the dais stands a great bronze basin, green with age, filled with seawater, in which lie the coins the cult has taken from its victims: 1,800 cp, 900 sp, 400 gp, and 50 pp. **The water in the basin is cursed:** any living creature that puts a hand in it must save vs. Spells or be unable to breathe air for 1 turn, as if drowning (2 hp damage per round; the curse ends if the victim plunges his head into seawater, where he can breathe normally until the turn is up).

Among the belongings on the pillars are trinkets worth a total of 250 gp, if the characters spend 3 turns searching (each extra searcher saves a turn). The DM should describe one or two as poignant (a child's wooden doll, a love letter, a seaman's ring engraved with a name).

If the alarm has been raised, the **4 acolytes** (see I4) will be here, ready to fight, with 2 zombies from I5.

**I4. ACOLYTES' QUARTERS.** (Room 30' × 30', door west to I3.) Four bare stone sleeping-shelves, four chests of personal gear, a table, and a shrine-niche with a small black stone fish. The **4 acolytes** of the Drowned Mother live here: shaven-headed men and women in gray-green robes, each wearing a black stone fish on a cord. They are fanatics, and fight to the death if the Tidecaller is with them.

*4 Acolytes (AC 5 (chain under robes), C1, hp 7, 6, 5, 4, #AT 1 mace, D 1d6, MV 90' (30'), Save C1, ML 9; spell: cause fear or darkness)*

The chests hold clothing, prayer-cords of shells, and a total of 88 gp. Under the table, in a hollow, is a small bag of 6 black pearls (worth 100 gp each), skimmed by one of the acolytes from the offerings and hidden from the Tidecaller.

**I5. THE POOL OF RISING.** (Cave 50' × 50', broad passage south to I3; the floor is a great round pool 30' across, 15' deep, with a rim of rock 10' wide. The Drowned Crack from H3 surfaces at the bottom of the pool's north side.)

<div class="box" markdown="1">
A dim green light rises from the water of a great round pool, glowing faintly, like moonlight under the sea. In the depths, shapes sway gently back and forth — pale, drifting shapes with arms and legs, standing upright on the bottom like men waiting in a crowd. As you watch, one of them turns its face up toward you.
</div>

This is where the Tidecaller raises the drowned. The bodies of sailors, fishermen, and sacrifices are laid in the pool, and after three nights of prayer, they rise as brine dead. At the bottom of the pool, standing in rows, are **8 brine dead** and **4 zombies** — the beginnings of the Tidecaller's army. They will rise from the pool and attack anyone who enters the room without wearing a black stone fish, 2 per round, beginning in the round after the intruders enter. Those who come up through the Drowned Crack (H3) will surface in the middle of them.

*8 Brine dead (AC 7, HD 2, hp 14, 12, 11, 10, 9, 9, 8, 7, #AT 1 grab, D 1d6 + drowning grip, MV 90' (30'), swim 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 12)*
*4 Zombies (AC 8, HD 2, hp 13, 11, 9, 8, #AT 1 claw, D 1d8, MV 90' (30'), Save F1, ML 12)*

This is a very dangerous room. The DM should give the players fair warning (the shapes in the pool, the green light) and let them choose whether to fight or to flee and bar the way. The undead will not pursue beyond I3 unless commanded to by the Tidecaller. A cleric's Turn Undead works normally on them. The green glow comes from a phosphorescent slime on the bottom of the pool; it is harmless.

**I6. CRYPT OF THE OLD PRIESTS.** (Room 40' × 30', door east to I3; a corpse-chute from J4 opens in the ceiling of the north end, above a heap of bones.) This ancient crypt holds a dozen stone niches, each containing the bones of a priest of the old wrecker-folk, wrapped in rotted nets and crowned with shells. In the center of the room is a heap of fresh bones, fallen from the chute in the ceiling; the cult dumps the bodies it does not need into J4 for the ghouls, and the ghouls drop the bones down here.

In the niches are **6 skeletons**, the old priests themselves, who rise to defend their crypt against anyone but the Tidecaller.

*6 Skeletons (AC 7, HD 1, hp 6, 6, 5, 4, 3, 3, #AT 1 bronze knife, D 1d6, MV 60' (20'), Save F1, ML 12)*

Each skeleton wears a crown of shells set with a single pearl (worth 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, and 100 gp). The chute in the ceiling is 3' wide and climbs at a steep slope for 50' to J4; climbing it is slippery and foul work (thieves at –10%; others need a rope).

**I7. ADEPTS' CELLS.** (Two small rooms, each 20' × 20', opening off a short passage that runs east from I8.) The Tidecaller's two senior followers, **Mother Kell** and **Brother Grue**, sleep here. If not already with the Tidecaller, they are at prayer in their cells.

*Mother Kell (AC 4, C2, hp 11, #AT 1 mace, D 1d6, MV 90' (30'), Save C2, ML 9; spell: cause light wounds)*
*Brother Grue (AC 4, C2, hp 10, #AT 1 trident, D 1d6, MV 90' (30'), Save C2, ML 9; spell: darkness)*

Kell's cell holds a chest with 140 gp and a silver-and-coral holy symbol of the Drowned Mother (worth 120 gp; evil). Grue's cell holds 95 gp, a *potion of healing* (which he will drink if wounded), and a diary in which he describes, gleefully, his part in the drowning of the *Merrow*'s crew.

**I8. THE TIDECALLER'S CHAMBERS.** (Room 40' × 30', doors to the passage to I7 in the east wall, to I9 in the north wall, and to I10 in the west wall; a stair in the northwest corner descends 20' to I11.) This is the private chamber of **Sedge Harrow, the Tidecaller**. It is richly furnished with plunder from wrecks: a carpet, a great bed of carved oak, a table of black wood, silver lamps burning fish-oil, and shelves of strange things in jars.

The Tidecaller is here unless he has been alerted, in which case he has gone to the Sanctuary (I11). Sedge Harrow is a tall, gaunt man of fifty with a shaven head, deep-set eyes, and a calm, gentle voice. The Tidecaller fights intelligently. He will use *darkness* to blind the most dangerous fighter, *cause fear* on a cleric or another leader, and command any undead present to attack spellcasters first. His *Trident of the Deep* (see New Magic Items) is a magic weapon that can call the water to drown his enemies. He is always accompanied by **2 brine dead** bodyguards.

*2 Brine dead bodyguards (AC 7, HD 2, hp 15, 13, #AT 1 grab, D 1d6 + drowning grip, MV 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 12)*

On the table are the Tidecaller's papers: letters from Black Maudie and Corwin Pell (much like those in D7), a list of names in Kingsreach marked with small fish symbols (the cult's few sympathizers — Brother Silt, Corwin Pell, and one other name the DM may choose, to be the seed of a future adventure), and a **tide calendar** for the coming months, in which the next spring tide is circled in black ink and labeled "*The Feast.*" This is the night the Tidecaller plans his attack (see *Concluding the Adventure*).

A locked chest at the foot of the bed holds 600 gp, 200 pp, a gold chalice set with sea-green gems (worth 1,200 gp), and a *scroll of hold person*. The key is around the Tidecaller's neck.

**I9. THE SCRIPTORIUM.** (Room 30' × 20', door south to I8.) Shelves of rotting books and scrolls, many salvaged from wrecks, line the walls; a desk holds ink, quills, and parchment. Most of the books are worthless, water-damaged ledgers and almanacs. Among them, however, are three of value: an old bestiary of sea monsters (worth 100 gp to a sage), a book of sea charts of the western coasts (worth 150 gp), and an ancient, crumbling scroll in the language of the old wrecker-folk, which the Tidecaller has been translating. His notes, on the desk, explain that the Drowned Mother is "not dead, but dreaming, in the deep beyond the Throat," and that "when ten hundred souls are given her at a single tide, she will wake." The DM may use this as the seed of a later, greater adventure.

**I10. THE HOLDING CELL.** (Room 20' × 20', door east to I8, barred on the outside.) The cult keeps its living sacrifices here, chained to the walls. At present, there is one prisoner: **Private Edwin Hale** (*AC 9, F1, hp 3 (normally 7)*), a young man-at-arms from the Kingsreach garrison, who was taken by the wreckers when he went fishing off Kingsreach three nights ago and sold to the cult. The garrison believes him drowned. He is to be sacrificed at the next spring tide. If rescued, he will fight beside the characters (he has no weapons, but will take any offered) and the Castellan will be deeply grateful. Edwin has overheard the cultists talking of "the Lamp" who "will raise the chain and the water-gate for the Mother's children."

**I11. THE SANCTUARY OF THE DROWNED MOTHER.** (Great cave 70' × 50', 40' high, reached by a stair from I8 that descends along the east wall. The southern half of the floor is a ledge of carved stone 20' above a great pool of seawater that fills the northern half; steps lead down from the ledge into the water. The pool is 30' deep and connects through an underwater opening on its north side with the Mother's Throat, I12.)

<div class="box" markdown="1">
The stair descends into a vast, echoing cave. Its floor is a broad ledge of carved stone, and beyond the ledge lies a great black pool that rises and falls gently with the breathing of the sea. On the far side of the pool, carved in the living rock of the cave wall and rising from the water to the roof, is a colossal figure: a woman with a calm, sleeping face and eight long, coiling arms that reach out across the walls in every direction. In the center of her brow, where a third eye might be, a single enormous pearl glows with a cold, black light. The water laps at her breast. You have the terrible feeling that she is about to open her eyes.

This is the heart of the cult. The great carving of the Drowned Mother rises 40' from the pool; it is not alive, and cannot move, but it radiates a deep and terrible evil. All undead within this cave are turned as if they were one Hit Die higher, and the Tidecaller's spells cannot be resisted by anyone who has touched the water of the pool (saves are made at –2).

If the Tidecaller has been alerted, he is here, standing on the lowest step at the water's edge, with his 2 brine dead bodyguards, the 2 adepts, and any acolytes or undead that are left. He will fight from the steps, using the Trident's power to raise a wave against the characters. If he is reduced to fewer than 5 hit points, he will throw himself into the pool and swim for the Throat (I12), trusting the Mother to save him. Whether she does is up to the DM — but he is a man, not a fish, and the Throat is long.

**The Eye of the Mother** is a black pearl the size of a man's fist, set 30' up in the carving's brow. Reaching it requires a climb, a flight, or a very good shot with a grapple. It is worth 3,000 gp — but it is the focus of the Mother's power in this world. As long as it is in place, the brine dead of the Gullet will rise again one week after being destroyed (unless their bodies are burned or given proper burial). If the pearl is pried from the carving, the undead in the Chapel collapse at once, and the carving's sleeping face seems to *frown*. The character who takes the pearl must save vs. Spells; if the save fails, he will dream of drowning every night, and will not regain hit points from rest until the pearl is either cast into the deepest sea or brought to Mother Wenna, who can purify it (in which case it becomes a holy relic worth 3,000 gp to the Church, and the dreams stop). Destroying the pearl (it can be shattered by a blow from a blessed or magical weapon) ends the cult's power in the Gullet forever — and earns the characters each an extra 200 XP.

At the bottom of the pool, 30' down, lies a scatter of offerings from centuries of worship: gold and silver coins and jewelry tarnished green, worth 2,000 gp in all, but scattered across the whole bottom of the pool. Recovering it requires 6 hours of diving (with the danger of the tide and of anything else that lives in the Throat).

**I12. THE MOTHER'S THROAT.** (An underwater tunnel 10' wide and about 300' long, running from the north side of the pool in I11 out under the cliff to a hole in the seabed at the foot of the Gullet's head, 30' below the surface at low tide.) At ordinary low tides, the Throat is entirely underwater. At a **spring low tide**, the seaward mouth is only 10' below the surface, and an air pocket forms in a chamber halfway along the tunnel (100' from the sea), so that a strong swimmer with no armor can make it through in two stages (each requiring a Constitution check, with failure meaning 1d6 damage from near-drowning and having to turn back). With *water breathing* magic or the pearls of the merfolk, the Throat can be swum at any time.

Lurking in the air-pocket chamber is a **giant moray eel**, which attacks anyone passing.

*Giant moray eel (AC 6, HD 3, hp 15, #AT 1 bite, D 1d8 (hangs on for 1d4 automatic damage each round thereafter), MV swim 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 9; see New Monsters)*

The Throat is the way the Tidecaller sends his brine dead out into the sea, and the way they will go, on the night of the Feast, to swim around the point to the Sea Gate. It is also the Tidecaller's last escape route.

## J. The Hall of the Lost Crews

At the northern end of the Shelf, near the head of the Gullet, a narrow cave mouth breathes out a sickly, sweetish smell that makes the goblins and kobolds give it a wide berth. Gulls will not land near it. This is where the cult throws the bodies it does not need, and something feeds on them.

**J1. THE BONE PORCH.** (Cave 20' × 30', entrance from the Shelf to the south, passage north to J2.) The floor is littered with bones, many of them human, gnawed and cracked for the marrow. Torn clothing, broken boots, and empty purses are scattered among them. A search (1 turn) reveals 3d6 sp and a brass compass (worth 10 gp).

**J2. THE CHARNEL CAVE.** (Cave 40' × 40', passage south to J1, passage east to J3, passage north to J4.) **3 ghouls** — once sailors of the *Silver Wren*, who ate the flesh of their dead shipmates when they were cast up in the Gullet, and became what they are — feed here on the latest remains. They are hideous, gray-skinned things with long, black-nailed fingers and the tattered rags of seamen's clothes. Their touch paralyzes.

*3 Ghouls (AC 6, HD 2*, hp 13, 10, 8, #AT 2 claws / 1 bite, D 1d3 each + paralysis, MV 90' (30'), Save F2, ML 9)*

Any character hit by a ghoul (except an elf) must save vs. Paralysis or be paralyzed for 2d4 turns. This is a deadly encounter for low-level characters, and the DM should let the smell, the bones, and the silence warn the players. The ghouls will not attack anyone wearing a black stone fish.

**J3. THE BOSUN'S NEST.** (Cave 20' × 20', passage west to J2.) The largest and oldest of the ghouls, once the *Silver Wren*'s bosun, keeps his hoard here: things that glitter, taken from the dead. He is crouched over his treasure, crooning to it, and will attack anyone who comes near.

*Ghoul bosun (AC 6, HD 3*, hp 17, #AT 2 claws / 1 bite, D 1d4 each + paralysis, MV 90' (30'), Save F3, ML 10)*

The hoard is a heap of 340 gp, 70 pp, a dozen rings (worth a total of 180 gp), a silver bosun's whistle (worth 25 gp; it is a *whistle of the winds* — see New Magic Items), and the *Silver Wren*'s logbook, which records that she was "lured by a light upon the eastern cliffs."

**J4. THE CHUTE.** (Cave 20' × 20', passage south to J2; in the floor, a hole 3' across opens onto a steep, slippery chute descending 50' to the crypt at I6.) The cultists bring the bodies they do not want up through the crypt and push them up the chute with poles for the ghouls (and the ghouls drop the bones back down). Climbing down the chute is possible with a rope, but very foul; anyone sliding down without a rope arrives in I6 in a heap of bones, taking 1d4 damage, and wakes the skeletons there.
